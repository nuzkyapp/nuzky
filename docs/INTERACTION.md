# Interaction spec

How the editor behaves. Visual tokens live in [DESIGN.md](../DESIGN.md).

## Layout

Nuzky starts on the home screen with every project; the editor of the last project is open behind it (see Home screen). The editor: top bar (Projects, name and save state in the middle, AI run, background jobs, AI, undo/redo, Export) · left panel (Media, Audio, Text, Captions, Transcript, Transitions, Filters) · preview with transport and Ratio · inspector · timeline across the bottom, its main track starting with the Cover tile · the AI panel, when open, as a column at the right edge by default. The layout follows CapCut so its users find everything where they expect it. Each control exists once: the canvas format lives in Ratio under the preview, the timecode in the transport.

The library, the inspector and the timeline start close to CapCut's proportions (28 % of the width each, 35 % of the height) and follow the window until resized. Drag the gap right of the library, left of the inspector or above the timeline to resize that pane (a grip shows while the pointer is over it); double-click resets it to its share of the window, and ←/→ (↑/↓ above the timeline) on the focused gap resize by 24 px. The preview always keeps at least 340 px of width and 300 px of height: a pane stops there, and in a smaller window the inspector gives way first, then the library; both get their size back when the window grows. The sizes are remembered.

## Response time

| Action | Feedback |
|---|---|
| Click, toggle, select | Visual state change in the same frame |
| Seek or scrub | Last frame stays on screen until the new one arrives, so the preview never flashes |
| Import | Item appears at once with a skeleton thumbnail; audio preparation shows a thin progress bar on the item |
| Import of video that decodes slowly (HEVC from phones, AV1, VP9, larger than 1080p) | A lighter copy for the preview is made in the background, one file at a time: "Preparing preview of IMG_0042.MOV 42%" with Stop in the top bar. Until it is ready the preview plays the original; from the next pause on it plays the copy, which has the same frames at the same times. Thumbnails made after that come from it too. Export always reads the original. A failed copy says so in a toast and the preview keeps the original |
| Export, captions, transcript | Background job with percentage and phase in the top bar; the user keeps editing; a toast reports the result |
| Long work an AI agent started (transcription, export, finding the person, scanning) | The same top bar capsule: what it does and its percentage, measured as it goes (a transcription counts the speech recognised, then the words timed), with Stop. Stop cancels only that work within seconds; the agent sees it stopped and says so |
| Sound library search, preview, add | Searching shows skeleton rows at once; a sound that has to download shows a spinner on its picture (preview) or the percentage on its + (add), and the panel stays usable |

## Flows

### Import
Entry: Import button, `Ctrl+I`, or files dropped on the window. Files dropped on the timeline are also placed on it; while media files are dragged over the timeline, an `accent` line shows where they would land. Each picked or dropped file shows at once in Media (and Audio for sound files) with its name and a skeleton thumbnail while it is read. The Audio tab's "Add music or sound", under In project, opens the picker filtered to audio.
Outcome: items appear in Media (all kinds) and, for audio, in Audio. Unsupported files produce one toast naming each file by name only with a short reason ("build.log is not a video, audio or image file"); the rest still import.
Hovering or focusing a media or audio item shows `+` (add at playhead), on videos and images a picture-in-picture button (see Add to timeline), and a trash button (remove the item and its clips at once; a toast offers Undo, like deleting clips).

### Add to timeline
`+` on a media item adds it at the playhead (video and images on the main track, audio on an audio track). Dragging an item onto a track places it at the pointer. The new clip is selected.

**Picture in picture**: the button on a video or image item puts it at the playhead in a small window over the main track, for a screenshot or a second camera beside a talking head. The window's longer side is 45 % of the canvas's shorter side; it sits in the top right corner, against the corner of the Reels and TikTok safe area on vertical videos and 4 % of the shorter side from the edges on the others, with corners rounded at 15 % and a soft shadow. It goes on the first overlay track free there, else on a new one above the others. A video plays its whole length, an image lasts 3 s. One undo step; the new clip is selected, so the inspector shows its crop and shape.

### Sound library
The Audio tab has three tabs: In project (the project's own sound files, with Add music or sound), Music and Sound effects. It opens on In project and remembers the last one. Their searches show only sounds under CC0 or CC BY, the licences that allow monetized videos; anything else a catalogue returns never shows. The one exception is a song the user links from YouTube themselves (below).

Sound effects lists the effects built into Nuzky (CC0, they work offline) with chips for their kinds (Whoosh, Transition, Pop and click, Bell and alert, Impact, Funny, Everyday) that scroll with the list. Typing filters them at once and, after a short pause or Enter, also searches online; online results follow under Online. Music has nothing built in: it offers ideas (Upbeat, Chill…) and searches online only when the user types or picks one; nothing is sent before. Online search uses Openverse (its Freesound and Wikimedia Commons sounds; music is searched by its words, as almost no other music there is filed as music; Jamendo's tracks stay out while its commercial terms are unsettled), or for sound effects Freesound when the user saved their own API key under Where to search (the sliders button beside the field; `Esc` closes it and returns there) and turned it on; the switch stays disabled until a key is saved. Only the typed text is sent. The results end with "Search uses Openverse. Nuzky is not endorsed or certified by Openverse." and the advice to check a sound's page, since catalogue licences can be wrong; More results loads the next page.

A row shows the title, the author and where it comes from (or the kind for a built-in one), the licence (CC BY or CC0) and the length. Clicking a row (or its picture, the keyboard's way) plays the sound through the app's own audio, downloading it first when needed (a spinner meanwhile); the row stays highlighted while it plays. Clicking again, another sound, Play on the timeline or leaving the tab stops it, and the timeline pauses while it plays. The licence opens the sound's page at its source in the browser. `+` on hover or focus, or a double click, adds the sound at the playhead on an audio track as one undo step, downloading it first (the `+` shows the percentage; clicking it stops the download, a double click does not). Library sounds are added with `+` or a double click, not by dragging. The file goes to Nuzky's sound library beside the projects, and the asset remembers the source, author, licence and page. A sound the project already has shows a check and adds another clip of the same file. While the AI is editing, `+` is disabled with the reason; playing and searching still work.

When a CC BY sound plays on the timeline, the bottom of the Audio tab says how many need credit, with Copy credits: it copies, for the video's description, each CC BY sound's title, author, page, licence with its link and what the video changed ("Synced to video; trimmed, faded."), then the CC0 ones as a courtesy, and a toast says where to paste it. The project's own CC BY audio shows a CC BY chip. Export writes the same text beside the video (see Export).

Offline, or when Openverse limits the searches, the online part says so in place of results with Try again (in Sound effects one line under the built-in sounds, which still show). A failed download says why in a toast and adds nothing.

Under the search field, Music has a field to paste a link to one YouTube or YouTube Music video (youtube.com, music.youtube.com, youtu.be, Shorts). Pasting into the empty field, or Enter, looks the video up; `Esc` clears it. Nuzky does not include a downloader: it uses yt-dlp, its own copy when it has one, otherwise the one installed on the computer, found on the `PATH` or where Homebrew, pipx, winget and Scoop put it. It gives yt-dlp only a watch address built from the video's id, never the pasted text. Without yt-dlp the line under the field says that music from YouTube comes through it and offers Download yt-dlp: Nuzky downloads the build for this computer from yt-dlp's newest release on GitHub, about 20 to 40 MB, with the percentage and Stop, checks it against the size and SHA-256 GitHub gives for it, keeps it beside the sound library and looks the link up again by itself. When the yt-dlp installed on the computer fails to look up or download a video for a reason Nuzky can't name, as when YouTube refuses an outdated one, the line says it is probably out of date and offers Download yt-dlp the same way; Play or `+` on the row then shows that line in place of the row, without an error toast; once the download finishes, Nuzky plays or adds the song as asked, and the downloaded copy is used from then on. A stopped or broken-off download continues where it stopped the next time. Clearing or replacing the link stops it. Once a week, after yt-dlp has run, Nuzky looks in the background for a newer release and downloads it when there is one, as YouTube often breaks an old one; meanwhile and offline it uses the copy it has. A look that failed is repeated after ten minutes of use. The very first time, before anything is looked up, a dialog says that Nuzky downloads only the sound, that the user is responsible for the rights to the music and for following YouTube's terms, and that copyrighted music can get a video muted, blocked or demonetized; Cancel looks nothing up, I understand looks the link up and is remembered, so the dialog never comes again. While looking up, a skeleton row; then the video is a row like any other, with its title, the artist or channel · YouTube and its length, and no licence chip. Play downloads only its sound (AAC, as YouTube serves it, and never with a part missing) and plays it; `+` adds it at the playhead as music, with the percentage while it downloads and the same stop, and clearing or replacing the link stops it too. Once the project has it, the row shows the check and `+` adds another clip of the same file. The file goes to the sound library like the other sounds, and the asset has no credit, so Copy credits and the credits file leave it out. Lower under speech, volume, fades and the export's loudness levelling work on it as on any music. A link that is not one video, a playlist, a private, removed or age-restricted video, a live stream, a video too long for a sound (over 60 MB of sound, about an hour), no internet, YouTube refusing too many requests and a download of yt-dlp that did not check out each give one line under the field saying what to do, with Try again where trying again can help. Where Nuzky has no yt-dlp build for the computer, the line says to install it from yt-dlp's page. Nothing is added then.

### Edit clips
- **Select**: click. `Shift`-click or `Cmd`-click (`Ctrl`-click outside macOS) adds a clip to the selection or takes it out. Click empty space or press `Esc` to clear.
- **Keyboard**: the clips are one tab stop (the clip focused last, else the first selected, else the first on the main track). On a focused clip `←`/`→` go to the previous or next clip on its track and `↑`/`↓` to the nearest clip on the track above or below, without moving the playhead, and select the clip they reach, so `Delete`, `S`, `Q`, `W` and `Ctrl+D` act on the clip with focus. `Ctrl` with an arrow moves focus without selecting, to build a selection with `Shift+Enter` (adds or removes the focused clip); `Enter` selects only it. `Alt+←`/`Alt+→` nudge it a frame (`Shift`: a second), stopping at its neighbours; on the main track they trade places with the clip beside it. A burst of nudges is one undo step. The Menu key or `Shift+F10` opens the clip's context menu; closing the menu returns focus to the clip. When the focused clip is deleted, focus and selection move to the clip that took its place on the track, else the one before it. `Space` works as everywhere.
- **Move**: drag the clip body. A ghost follows the pointer and snaps within 8 px to the playhead and clip edges; a vertical line shows the snap. Dropping on an occupied spot creates a new track. `Esc` cancels the drag, and so does the system taking the pointer or the window losing focus.
- **Main track**: magnetic. Clips sit back to back; moving reorders, deleting closes the gap. While a clip is dragged the track shows the result live: the neighbours move aside, a dashed slot and an `accent` bar mark where it goes in, and the gap it left closes. Trimming a main-track clip keeps its start and moves the clips after it with the edge. The other tracks move with it, so captions, titles and overlays stay over the same picture: the time the edge gains opens on them too and the time it loses is cut from them, as with `Q` and `W`; audio tracks set to keep in place (see Transcript) are left alone. Media dragged over the main track marks the cut it would go in at.
- **Trim**: drag a clip edge. A tooltip shows the new duration. The handle stops at the end of the source media, taking the clip's speed into account.
- **Fade**: a clip on an audio track has a dot in each top corner, shown while the pointer is over the clip or it is selected; while there is no fade it covers the top of the trim handle, so trimming takes the edge below it. Drag the left dot right to fade the sound in from the start, the right dot left to fade it out to the end, as in CapCut. The dot stays at the end of the fade, the clip shows the fade as it changes, and a tooltip its length ("Fade in 1.2 s"). Fades go in steps of 0.1 s up to half the clip or 10 s, whichever is shorter, and each drag is one undo step. `Esc` cancels the drag and leaves the fade as it was. The inspector's Audio tab sets the same fades from the keyboard, and the fades of a video clip's own sound. While the AI is editing, the dots are hidden.
- **Split**: at the playhead. `S` and the Split button split the selected clips under the playhead, else the main-track clip under it, and are disabled with the reason when there is none; the context menu splits the selected clips under the playhead.
- **Cuts sound clean**: where two pieces of sound meet that do not play on from each other, they crossfade over 20 ms around the cut (10 ms on each side, reaching past the clips only into quiet sound of the files), so no cut clicks or dips; where the sound past the cut is not quiet, such as the start of a deleted word, the two sides fade out and in over 5 ms instead, so nothing deleted is heard. Split halves that play on, and cuts with a transition, are left as they are.
- **Delete left / right**: `Q` and `W` or the buttons next to Split remove the part of the clip left or right of the playhead, CapCut's fast way to cut a talking head. They act on the selected clips under the playhead, else the main-track clip under it. On the main track the time is cut from every track, so captions, titles and overlays stay in sync; audio tracks set to keep in place (see Transcript) are left alone. A clip on another track is trimmed instead. After `Q` the playhead sits on the cut. Each press is one undo step.
- **Duplicate**: `Ctrl+D`, the Duplicate button or the context menu. The copy goes right after the original and is selected.
- **Copy, cut and paste**: `Ctrl+C` or the context menu copies the selected clips, also from several tracks, and a toast says how many; `Ctrl+X` cuts them, with Undo in the toast. `Ctrl+V` or Paste at playhead pastes them at the playhead keeping their offsets to each other. The clips of each track go back on it when they all fit there, else on a new track right above it, as when dragging; a track a cut left empty is gone, so its clips go on the first free track of their kind, else a new one. Clips from the main track go in back to back at its cut nearest the playhead and push the main-track clips after it along, while the other tracks stay where they are; every other pasted clip keeps its offset to the main-track clip it starts over, so a take keeps its sound and titles also when the pasted takes close a gap between them. The pasted clips are selected and the paste is one undo step. The clipboard is Nuzky's own, never the system's: it keeps the clips as they were copied, and opening another project empties it, as their media belongs to the project. With nothing copied, `Ctrl+V` says so in a toast and Paste at playhead is disabled with that reason. With text selected, such as an error message, `Ctrl+C` copies the text. Copying works while the AI is editing; cutting and pasting are refused like other edits.
- **Paste attributes**: `Ctrl+Alt+V` or the context menu gives the selected clips the look of the copied clip of their kind (with several copied, the earliest one): a text takes its style, font, transform, keyframes and animations and keeps its own words (a caption keeps its wrap width); a video or image takes its Adjust, Background, Crop and shape, Motion and animations and keeps its place and size, the motion zooming from its own framing over its own length; an audio clip takes its volume, fades, Voice and Lower under speech. What the copied clip does not have, such as a background or an animation, the others lose. Clips of another kind are left as they were. It is one undo step, and a toast says how many clips changed, how many of another kind were left, and offers Undo. With no clip of the copied kind selected, it is disabled with the reason, which the toast also gives for the keys.
- **Delete**: `Delete`, `Backspace`, the toolbar button or the context menu; all of them show a toast with Undo. With a transition selected they remove the transition.
- **Context menu**: right-click a clip for Split, Duplicate, Detach audio, Select all on track, Cut, Copy, Paste at playhead, Paste attributes and Delete. Right-clicking a clip outside the selection selects it first; every item then acts on the selection, and with several clips selected the labels say how many (“Delete 3 clips”). Select all on track uses the clicked clip's track. Unavailable items are disabled with the reason in a tooltip; ↑/↓ reach them too and show the reason. ↑/↓ move, Esc closes.
- **Undo/redo**: `Ctrl+Z`, `Ctrl+Shift+Z` or `Ctrl+Y`. A slider drag, a typing burst, a preview drag or a caption restyle is one step. Undo in a toast undoes the step the toast names; once another change follows it, the toast no longer offers Undo.
- **Versions**: the clock button after Undo and Redo lists the newest 20 versions of the project, newest first: what made each (an AI run's label with a sparkle, or the step, such as Split, Undo or Restore) and when. A version is the project after each kept AI run, each of your steps, each undo, redo and restore, and as it was opened. They stay after a restart, up to the newest 200. The current one has a check. Choosing another restores it as one step, and a toast offers Undo; the versions after it stay in the list. `↑`/`↓` move, `Enter` restores, `Esc` closes. While the AI is editing, the button is disabled with the reason.
- **Order**: edits, undo and redo apply one at a time in the order they were made. An edit made before the previous one is confirmed builds on it, so two quick changes (two sliders, two keyframes) both stick.

### Timeline display
Clips are drawn 1 px in from each side so every cut is visible. Video clips show a filmstrip whose frames match the source position under each tile, speed included (a single repeated thumbnail until the filmstrip is ready), and a thin waveform strip at the bottom while they have sound. A waveform draws a bar every 3 px of what is on screen, rising from the bottom edge as loud as the sound is there on a decibel scale, so a pause reads as a low line; zoomed in further than the sound's detail, the bars blend from one peak to the next instead of stepping. While a file's sound is still being prepared after import, its waveform fills in from the start as the sound is decoded; parts not decoded yet stay empty. Clips not at 1x carry a speed badge ("2x"). Thin bars at the top edge show the length of the entry (left) and exit (right) animation. A sound fade on an audio clip shows as a line from the bottom corner up to where the fade ends, with the corner above it shaded. The selected clip shows its keyframes as diamonds; clicking one, or `Enter` on a focused one, moves the playhead there. Caption clips show only their text. When sound runs past the last picture or text, the video ends there: a dashed line on the ruler and lanes marks the end, the time after it is dimmed, and the ruler's tooltip says the sound after it is not exported.

### Transitions
Every cut on the main track has a small square. Clicking an empty one adds a 0.5 s Dissolve and selects it; clicking a set one selects it. A selected transition opens in the inspector with its name, duration and Remove; the kind is picked in the Transitions tab. The Transitions tab applies to the selected cut, else the cut before the selected main-track clip, else the cut nearest the playhead, and names that cut. Duration is capped at 2 s and by the shorter neighbouring clip. None removes the transition.

### Filters
The Filters tab applies a preset (None, Vivid, Warm, Cool, Mono, Fade, Moody, Punch) to the selected video or image clip. A preset replaces the clip's Adjust values; the matching preset is marked. Without a media clip selected the tiles are disabled with the reason.

### Playback
`Space` plays and pauses; the focused control keeps focus and is not pressed. A focused checkbox or menu item keeps Space for itself. Clicking or dragging the ruler scrubs. `←`/`→` step one frame, with `Shift` one second. `Home`/`End` jump to the ends. A focused slider keeps its own arrow, Home and End keys. The sound card drives the clock, so picture and sound stay in sync. Mute in the transport (`M`) silences the preview on this computer only: the picture plays on, the project and the export keep their sound, and the choice is remembered. Full screen (`F`) shows the preview with its transport over the whole screen, drawn in more pixels; `F`, `Esc` or the button leave it, and a window that was in full screen before stays in it. There the keys play, seek, mute and leave, while the keys that would change the hidden timeline (cut, split, delete, duplicate, paste, import, undo and redo) rest, and an AI run shows its bar with Stop above the video.

### Preview
Clicking the paused preview selects the top-most visible layer under the pointer, so a click outside the curve of a circle or a rounded corner reaches the layer below; clicking where no layer is clears the selection. The selected layer gets a box: drag inside it to move, drag a corner to scale uniformly, drag the round handle to rotate (`Shift` snaps to 15°). On videos and images, dragging the bar in the middle of an edge crops that edge: the rest of the picture stays where it is, a dashed outline shows the whole picture while dragging, and each edge stops 5 % short of the opposite one. An edge too short on screen for its bar to clear the corner handles has none; the inspector crops it. The box of a layer larger than the frame is cut off at the edge of the preview area, and handles that would leave it stay on that edge, so they can always be grabbed. While moving, the layer centre snaps to the canvas centre lines within 6 px and a guide shows. Each gesture is one undo step. If the clip has keyframes, the gesture writes the keyframe at the playhead. Ratio, right of the transport, switches the canvas between 9:16, 16:9, 1:1 and 4:5; in its menu ↑/↓ move through the formats and their Reframe buttons, and choosing or Esc returns focus to Ratio.

**Reframe**, beside each format in the Ratio menu (shown while its row is hovered or has keyboard focus), changes the canvas to that format and places the picture so the speaker's face stays in it: in the selected video and image clips, or with none selected in every one on the main track and any other that filled the frame (a picture in picture is left alone). It looks for the face twice a second in each clip as recorded, in the background: a chip in the middle of the preview's bottom edge shows “Following the face · 42%” with Stop, which stops it within a second and changes nothing, and the first time “Downloading the face model” (230 KB). The picture fills the frame upright with the face across the middle of what Reels and TikTok leave free (on other formats of the frame) and 40 % down it, as far as the picture reaches, so no edge shows. It holds still while the face moves less than 8 % of the frame across or 6 % down and then catches up on a smooth curve over 0.6 s, following a face that keeps going in one move, so the picture never floats; one wrong detection never moves it. Only a face the clip's crop leaves visible is followed; a clip with no face anywhere is centred. Each clip keeps its zoom beyond filling the frame (a punch-in stays), its crop and opacity; its keyframes are replaced by the ones written, which edit like any others, and a cut jumps to the next clip's framing. The whole change is one undo step: when done the chip says “Reframed 4 clips” (and how many had no face) with Undo and Done, any later edit hides it, and closing it by keyboard returns focus to Ratio. When nothing needed to change, a toast says so and nothing is added to the history. An edit made while the face is being followed fails the reframe with “The project changed while following the face. Reframe again.”, and a missing file of a clip it moves fails it with what to do. Reframe is disabled with its reason while the timeline has no video or image and while a reframe runs.

### Inspector
Shows the selected clip with CapCut's tabs, remembering the last tab per clip kind:

| Selection | Tabs |
|---|---|
| Video clip | Video (transform, crop and shape, background, motion), Adjust, Speed, Animation, Audio with Voice (only when the file has sound) |
| Image clip | Image (transform, crop and shape, background, motion), Adjust, Animation |
| Text or caption clip | Text, Animation, Transform |
| Audio clip (also detached sound) | Audio: volume, fades, Lower under speech; Voice; speed |
| Transition | Kind name, duration, Remove |
| Nothing | Project: format (opens Ratio) and frame rate, background colour or blur with strength |
| Several clips | Duplicate, Delete, and the controls they all share: Transform, Font (text), Background and Adjust (video, images), Audio volume, Lower under speech and Voice (clips with sound) |

Adjust offers Exposure, Brightness, Contrast, Highlights, Shadows, Saturation, Temperature, Tint, Fade, and Vignette for single or multiple media clips.

While playing, values that follow keyframes update ten times a second; paused or scrubbing, they show the exact frame. Number fields keep what you type until Enter or blur, so "-" or "1," is never rejected mid-typing; Esc restores the value, ↑/↓ step (`Shift` ×10), commas work as decimal points. Changes preview live.

- **Keyframes**: the diamond in the Transform header adds a keyframe with the current values at the playhead, or removes the one there (filled diamond). Arrows jump to the previous and next keyframe. Once a clip has keyframes, every transform change updates or creates the keyframe at the playhead. A keyframe moves on to the next one at an even pace, or on a smooth curve when Motion wrote it; a new keyframe takes the curve of the one before it. Removing the last keyframe keeps its values. Reset clears the transform and all keyframes and keeps the crop at the playhead.
- **Crop and shape** (videos and images), right under Transform: Shape is Rectangle, Rounded (Corners from 1 to 99 % of half the shorter side, 15 % to start with, as a new picture in picture has) or Circle. Circle rounds fully and crops the longer side so a square is left, in the same undo step; the layer moves so the side nearer the canvas edge stays put, so a picture in picture in a corner stays in the corner and a centred layer stays centred. Crop left, right, top and bottom cut that share of the picture off, each stopping 5 % short of the opposite edge; the rest stays where it is. The crop is part of the transform: once the clip has keyframes, a crop change writes the keyframe at the playhead like the other transform values, so a crop can move, and a line says so. Border draws up to 40 px (canvas pixels) outside the edge in its color; Border color shows once it is wider than 0. Shadow puts a soft shadow under the layer, 0–100 %. Reset crop and shape shows the whole picture with plain edges again, every keyframe included. The preview and the export draw the same edge.
- **Background** (videos and images), below Crop and shape: None, Blur, Color or Image behind the person, like CapCut's background removal. Blur blurs the clip's own picture behind the person, Strength 5–100 % (50 % to start with), with the person left out of the blur, so the room fills in behind them instead of a halo of their colours; it is graded with the clip, Adjust included. Color (a dark grey to start with) and Image (an image of the project, covering the whole clip, so a crop or its move does not move it) show as picked, Adjust does not recolour them. Image takes the newest image of the project (never the clip's own picture), or asks for one to import when there is none; the image button, with a small picture of the image, lists the project's images and Import image…. Choosing the option already on keeps its strength, colour or image. The person is found once per file, in the frames the clips show, as a job, one file at a time: meanwhile "Finding the person · 42%" with a bar shows under the controls ("Waiting to find the person" while another file goes first), the top bar lists the job, and the preview shows those clips as recorded, switching part by part as the job gets through them, even while paused. A minute of 1080p video at 30 fps takes about 12 s, of 4K at 60 fps about 30 s. Export always has the background: it finds the person first when the job has not. Split, trimmed and copied clips of the file share what was found; another file, or the file changed, is looked at again. Choosing None, removing the clip or opening another project stops a job no clip needs any more; what it found stays. When the person model is missing a row says that showing and exporting the background needs it and offers to download it (1 MB); when the models cannot run on this computer the row says why and the options stay disabled. When the person cannot be found in a file, a toast and the row say why: the clip shows as recorded and None lets it export. The edge is a few pixels soft and follows the person from frame to frame without flickering; it suits one person filling much of the frame, as in a talking head. With several clips selected, a dash means they differ; a choice applies to all of them as one undo step.
- **Motion**: a slow camera move on a video or image clip in one click, below Background. Push in zooms in, Pull out starts zoomed in and ends on the clip's own framing, Ken Burns zooms in while drifting right; None removes the motion. A new move runs over the whole clip at the strength used last (10 % at first). Strength (6, 10 or 15 %) and Range rewrite the clip's move and are disabled with the reason until it has one. Range is the whole clip, or Rest of sentence: from the playhead to the end of the sentence said there, found in the transcript and kept inside the clip; without a transcript, or with the playhead off the clip, a toast says why and nothing changes. Picking Rest of sentence again moves the move to the sentence at the playhead. Another preset keeps the range of the move it replaces. The move starts and ends gently and is written as two ordinary keyframes, so Transform edits it like any keyframes, and each click is one undo step. On vertical videos it zooms about the middle of the Reels and TikTok safe area, otherwise about the centre of the frame, so a face framed there stays put and clear of the apps' buttons. The section marks the clip's move with its strength and range; keyframes the user changed by hand mark no preset. A preset on a clip with keyframes of its own replaces them, starting from the picture at the playhead, and None removes them keeping that picture; both show a toast that offers Undo. The tiles play their move while hovered or focused.
- **Several clips**: one change applies to every selected clip as one undo step, a slider drag included. Where the clips differ the field shows "—"; a typed value then applies to all. A transform change applies to the whole clip, every keyframe included, so "110 % on every clip" is Select all on track plus one value.
- **Speed**: presets 0.5x–3x or a logarithmic slider from 0.1x to 10x. Speed changes the clip length; the tab shows the new duration and the source length used. Keep pitch, a checkbox shown when the clip has sound and is not at 1x, keeps a voice as high as at 1x instead of higher when faster and lower when slower. Moving a clip off 1x turns it on; turned off, it stays off until the clip is back at 1x. Clips sped up in a project from before it keep the changed pitch until it is checked. Playback and export sound the same. Captions, word times and cuts stay where speed puts them, the sound stays within 11 ms of there, and sound cut away does not come back. A split whose halves differ in it crossfades like a cut.
- **Animation**: In and Out each take one preset with a duration; the selected preset is marked and the slot label shows a dot when set.
- **Audio**: volume, fade in and fade out (in steps of 0.1 s up to half the clip or 10 s, like the dots on the timeline), Lower under speech. Detach audio moves the sound to its own audio track and silences the video clip; disabled with the reason when there is nothing to detach.
- **Voice**, a section under Audio: Clean voice, Reduce echo, Even out volume and Match loudness, in the order the sound goes through them, all off by default and all for speech. They change how the clip sounds, never when: cuts, crossfades, captions and word times stay on the same sample, the waveform and the transcript keep the original sound, and projects without them sound exactly as before. Each file's sound for them is prepared once in the background, a row with a bar under the setting saying what runs ("Cleaning voice · 42%", "Reducing echo · 10%", and "Measuring loudness" under Match loudness for both gains), the top bar listing the job; playback keeps what is ready and switches without stopping. When a preparation fails, its row says why with a triangle and the clip plays without that setting; export needs it, so it works once the setting is off. The file is not tried again until Nuzky restarts. Export always has everything; it waits for the preparation or does it itself. Turning a setting off, removing the clip or opening another project stops a preparation no clip needs any more. A split whose halves differ in a Voice setting crossfades like a cut. With several clips selected, a dash means only some have a setting; checking turns it on for all of them as one undo step, keeping the strength of those that have it, and the strength shows once all have it.
- **Clean voice**: a checkbox for speech recorded on a phone. A high-pass at 80 Hz takes away rumble and 50/60 Hz hum, gentle noise reduction (RNNoise with 15 % of the original mixed back, so speech does not sound processed) lowers steady room noise by about 15 dB between words, and a de-esser softens harsh s sounds by up to 6 dB. With Reduce echo, the echo is taken out first and the voice cleaned after.
- **Reduce echo**: a checkbox for speech recorded in a room that rings, with Reduce by (1–100 %, 80 % to start with) under it once checked. The sound without the room's reverb is made once per file on the computer, without a model or the network: weighted prediction error (WPE, as nara_wpe) takes away the reverb that can be predicted from the sound before it, then late reverb suppression turns down what the room's decay leaves 50 ms after the sound, by the reverb time measured from the file's own decays. At 100 % what rings on after a phrase drops by about 10 dB from a phone's one microphone and about 20 dB from two, at 80 % by about 7 and 13 dB; the voice loses only the reverb it carried, 1–3 dB of level, and steady room tone comes down a few dB. Reduce by mixes that sound with the original, so changing it is instant.
- **Even out volume**: a checkbox that brings the quiet and loud passages of a recording together, for a speaker who turns away or leans in, with Even out by (1–100 %, 50 % to start with) under it once checked. Every moment is compared with the speech within 1 s either side and moved towards the file's loudness by that share, at 100 % by up to 12 dB; the gain changes over about a second, so it never pumps on words, and pauses keep the gain of the speech next to them. Match loudness, when on, is measured after it. Peaks stay under −1 dBFS as with Match loudness.
- **Match loudness**: a checkbox that plays every recording equally loud, at −16 LUFS (EBU R128), so takes from different rooms and distances match. It measures the whole file as the clip's other Voice settings make it sound, leaving out pauses, so every piece of one take gets the same gain and keeps the take's own ups and downs. Volume still applies on top, and the end of the line shows the clip's resulting gain, Match loudness and Volume together, in dB (+6.2 dB). It moves a file by at most 20 dB and never pushes a peak over −1 dBFS: where it would, the gain dips 40 ms before the peak and comes back over 250 ms. Export presets then level the whole video to −14 LUFS with their limiter last, a step of about 2 dB.
- **Lower under speech** (ducking): a checkbox for music under a talking head, off by default. Checked, the clip goes 12 dB down while someone speaks and comes back in the pauses; Lower by, shown under it, sets 1–40 dB. Speech is the sound of video clips, also sound detached from them, on unmuted tracks: any 10 ms of their file louder than -40 dBFS, so other loud sound in a video counts too, while imported audio files never do and a clip that lowers itself never lowers another. The clip starts going down 150 ms before the first word, so the word is clear, stays down through gaps between words up to 250 ms and comes back over 400 ms, without clicks. Playback and export sound the same, Reels levelling to -14 LUFS included; projects without it sound exactly as before. A split whose halves differ in it crossfades like a cut. With several clips selected, a dash means only some have it; checking turns it on at 12 dB for the others and keeps the strength of those that have it, as one undo step, and Lower by shows once all have it.

### Text and captions
Text presets add a text clip at the playhead, centred in the frame, and select it; captions sit a little below the middle (y +0.15, where reels put them). Transform Reset puts each back there. The inspector offers the same style presets under the same names as the Text tab, or the Captions tab for a caption. The inspector calls a caption clip "Caption".

**Font**: the Font field opens a searchable list. Fonts that ship with Nuzky come first, each name drawn in its own face; fonts installed on the computer follow. ↑/↓ move, Enter picks, Esc closes. Text without a font uses Inter, also on machines without it. A font the computer does not have shows a warning; the engine draws Inter instead. Presets carry no font, so the font survives a preset change.

**Captions**: choose language, accuracy, words per caption (1, 2, 3 or Phrases; 1–3 words also stay within 15 characters), style, key words and font, then Generate. Reel is the default style: Inter 95 px regular, white with a 7.5 px black outline, no box, 2 words. On vertical videos captions keep to the Instagram Reels and TikTok safe area (clear of the top bar, the like and comment rail and the caption and buttons at the bottom; 1080×1920: 250 px top, 180 px right, 500 px bottom, 60 px left): longer captions wrap instead of reaching under the app's interface, and restyling keeps that width. Captions keep a number with the word before it ("iPhone 17") and a sentence does not end on a lone word when the caption before can give one. A caption never runs across a cut. Cutting speech after the captions were made takes the cut words out of them: a caption with words left on both sides of a cut becomes two, one with no word left goes, and a caption whose text was typed over keeps its longer side whole. The first run downloads the model with progress, unless Captions model in Get set up downloaded it already. Recognition runs in the background and can be cancelled; while it runs the fields are locked and a starting job shows an indeterminate bar. Captions and Transcript share the language and accuracy and one recognition at a time. The language starts as the computer's language when it is offered (else Detect automatically); the language and the accuracy are remembered once picked, here or in Get set up. Generating again replaces the previous Captions track, keeps the look of the captions there (style, font, animations and key words), and the button label says so. With captions on the timeline, picking a style, key words or a font changes every caption at once, as one undo step; words per caption apply when you regenerate. Before there are captions, the choices wait for Generate.

**Caption styles**: a style is a whole look for reels: size, colours, outline or box, the entry and exit animation of every caption, a karaoke highlight and key words, and for some a font from the bundled fonts. Picking one applies all of it to every caption; a style without its own font keeps the font the captions have, so a font picked separately survives it. The style matching the captions is checked. A caption's inspector offers the same styles for that caption alone, animations included. Agents pick the same styles by name (`build_captions` `style_preset`).

**My styles**: + under Style saves the current look (the selected caption's, else the first caption's, else the look waiting for Generate) under a name typed in place; `Enter` saves, `Esc` cancels. A saved style is a tile like the built-in ones and applies the same way. Its … button on hover, a right click, or the context menu key on a focused tile offers Rename (also `F2`) and Delete; Delete shows a toast with Undo. Names are unique and never a built-in style's name; a refused name says why under the field, which stays open. Focus returns to the tile, or to + when there is none. Saving, renaming and deleting work while recognition runs, as they do not change the project; the AI lock stops them like every change. Generate captions stays in view under the choices. My styles are kept beside the projects, not in a project, so every project and an agent (`style_preset` by name) can use them, also after a restart.

**Key words**: Off, Emphasis, First, Last or Longest, with their colour. Emphasis colours the numbers and the words said louder than the rest, scored like the punch-in suggestions (`analyze(kind: "emphasis")`): how much louder than the median word, an exclamation, and a number; where a word sits in its sentence does not count, so some captions have none and key words lie at least 1.5 s apart. Words shorter than four letters are never key unless they are numbers. The words are scored once when the captions are made, from their sound (a file whose sound cannot be read counts numbers and exclamations only), so they stay key through a word correction, a split, a trim or a cut, and regenerating scores them again. First, Last and Longest mark that word of every caption instead. Like karaoke, a caption whose words were added or removed by hand colours none. The word being spoken takes the karaoke highlight over its key word colour, so the two combine. A caption's inspector offers Key words for that caption.

Saved style names are trimmed, contain 1–40 characters and are unique across saved and built-in packages ignoring case and treating underscores and hyphens as spaces. An unreadable or damaged styles file reports an error and is never overwritten when another style is saved.

**Karaoke**: the Karaoke style (Reel with the spoken word in yellow), Green box (bold white on a dark box, the spoken word green), Spotlight and White box light up the word being said. Each generated caption keeps when each of its words is heard; a word is drawn in the highlight colour from its start until its end, and nothing is lit between words, before the first or while the caption holds after the last. Only the colour changes, so the size and the line breaks stay put. Split, trim, `Q`/`W`, text cuts and Duplicate keep every word on its moment. Fixing words in a caption's text keeps the timing as long as it has as many words as before (a corrected typo lights up when the old word did); adding or removing a word turns the caption's highlight off, the inspector says so under Highlight colour, and regenerating brings it back. A caption's inspector offers Highlight spoken word with its colour.

### Stickers and emoji
The Text tab has three tabs: Text (the text presets), Stickers and Emoji. It opens on Text and remembers the last one. Stickers are Nuzky's own drawings: two arrows, a circle, an underline, a check, a cross, a heart, a star, sparkles, emphasis lines, a highlighter and a speech bubble. Emoji are Google's Noto emoji of Unicode 18 in their groups, with a button per group above the list that jumps to it and shows which group is in view. Typing in the search field finds emoji by name and by the words Unicode lists for them, so "fire" also finds the fire engine; Enter adds the first result, ↓ moves to the results and `Esc` clears the field. The swatch beside the field picks the skin tone of the emoji that have one, and Nuzky remembers it; emoji that mix two skin tones come only through `add_sticker`, from the emoji itself. In the grids the arrow keys move and Enter adds; the group buttons are one Tab stop, and ←/→ jump from group to group.

Clicking a sticker or an emoji adds it at the playhead as an image clip, centred, its longer side 30 % of the canvas's shorter side, for 3 s. It goes on the first overlay track free there, else on a new one above the others, and never moves the main track. One undo step; the new clip is selected, so the inspector shows its transform and animations, which work as on any image. The picture is drawn once at 1024 px into Nuzky's sticker library beside the projects and never changes afterwards, so a project keeps the same picture after an update brings new art; adding the same one again adds another clip of the same file. Nothing is downloaded: the art is built into Nuzky. While the AI is editing, adding is disabled with the reason; browsing, searching and the skin tone still work. Agents add stickers and emoji with `add_sticker`, by the emoji itself, its English name or a sticker's name.

### Transcript
Text-based editing, as in CapCut. Transcribe timeline recognises each video file whose sound plays on the timeline (music and other audio files are not listened to), once and in the file's own time; a captions run does the same and reuses what is already recognised. The transcript belongs to the file, so it follows every edit (cuts, trims, `Q`/`W`, speed, detached sound, mute, undo and redo), is still there after a restart and is reused when the file is in another project. The refresh button recognises every file again. After recognising Czech or English, the job measures where each word starts and ends in the sound ("Measuring word times"), so cuts in speech without pauses land between words; the first time, it downloads that language's word timing model with progress ("Downloading word timing model"), unless Captions model in Get set up downloaded it already. Without the model, for example offline, the transcript still comes, with word times estimated as before, and the toast says the word times are estimated. Cancel stops it within a second. When a clip's file has no transcript yet, a note says "1 clip not transcribed" with Transcribe; until then Delete, Remove pauses and Remove retakes and pauses are disabled with that reason. The words flow as text in paragraphs that break where speech pauses for a second or more, each led by its timecode. Pauses longer than the threshold (0.5 s by default) show as chips with their length. A sound between two words that recognition did not write, such as an "eee", shows as a hesitation chip with its length ("Hesitation 0.3 s") when it lasts 0.15 s or more and quiet parts it from both words, whatever the threshold; sound that runs straight into a word is taken for that word. Hesitation chips appear once the file's sound is prepared, and not in recordings where speech is not clearly louder than the room, such as over music. While playing, the current word is highlighted and kept in view.

- **Select**: click a word to jump there and select it; drag or `Shift`-click to extend. With the text focused, `←`/`→` move word by word (`Shift` extends), `Home`/`End` jump to the ends, `Enter` jumps to the selection, `Esc` clears it, `Space` plays without leaving the text.
- **Delete**: `Delete`, `Backspace` or the Delete button in the bar under the text, which shows the selection's word count and length. It cuts the selected words and the silence around them, keeping 0.12 s after the word before and 0.08 s before the word after, counted from where those words are heard in the sound, so a cut never clips a syllable, out of every track except audio kept in place, so captions and overlays stay in sync, as one undo step. A selection of pause and hesitation chips alone removes them; a hesitation goes like a deleted word, with 0.12 s and 0.08 s of the silence around it kept as for words. Hesitations selected together with words go with them. The playhead lands on the cut; a toast with the time removed offers Undo. While the bar shows, toasts sit above it so they never cover Delete. Inside the text these keys never delete timeline clips.
- **Remove pauses**: the button names the count and the time it removes ("Remove 5 pauses · 6.7 s") and cuts every pause chip at once. A pause between words keeps half the threshold next to each word, so a 2 s pause at 0.5 s becomes 0.5 s (where the next clip starts speaking at once, the whole pause stays after the last word of the clip before, so no sliver of a clip is left); silence longer than the threshold before the first word and after the last one is trimmed to 0.08 s and 0.12 s. Pauses and text cuts only lie inside clips that carry speech: clips in which words were recognised. A clip without words, such as B-roll, is never part of a pause and never cut from the text.
- **Remove retakes and pauses**: the prominent button under the pause row cleans up a talking head in one step, as an agent's rough cut does. It cuts the earlier attempts of every restarted sentence, keeping the last complete one, attempts that trail off before a new sentence starts, filler words and every hesitation and pause chip, as one undo step. Hesitation sounds that recognition wrote ("ehm", "hmm") go anywhere; words such as "jakoby" or "prostě" go at the start of a sentence when a comma or a pause of 0.15 s follows them, and inside one when commas or pauses set them apart on both sides ("Mluvte, jakoby, ke kamarádovi", not "Vypadá to jakoby snadno"). Filler words are looked for in Czech and English only, where they cannot mean something else (German "Um zehn Uhr"); in other languages the button cuts retakes, hesitation chips and pauses. Two sentences that differ in a negation, a number or a content word are not taken for attempts at one, and both stay. Words go with the silence around them as with Delete, pauses shrink as with Remove pauses, and the captions follow. A toast with Undo names what went and the time removed ("Removed 3 retakes, 2 filler words, 1 hesitation and pauses over 0.5 s (12.6 s)"). The button is disabled with the reason while clips are not transcribed and when there is nothing to remove.
- **Correct a word**: double-click a word, or press `F2` with one word selected, and type what was said; `Enter` or clicking elsewhere saves, `Esc` cancels. The word then reads that way in the transcript, in the captions that show it (their style and timing stay) and in captions generated again, also after a restart. The correction belongs to the project and to that recognised word: if the file is recognised again and the word comes out differently, it no longer applies. Typing the recognised text again removes it. A corrected word is underlined with dots and its tooltip says what was recognised. A toast offers Undo; one undo takes back the word and its captions. While AI is editing, a word cannot be corrected and the notice says why.
- **Suggest zooms**: the button next to the word count marks the sentences said with emphasis for a subtle punch-in: louder than the median sentence, an exclamation, or the opening hook; 1.2–8 s and three words or more, at least 5 s apart and about one per 12 s of timeline, each with its zoom (1.15–1.3×, more for a sentence that stands out more). A bar above the text says how many, with Dismiss and Apply N zooms. Apply takes focus when the suggestions arrive, `Esc` in the bar dismisses them, and focus then returns to Suggest zooms. Apply zooms in on every marked sentence as one undo step and a toast with the count offers Undo: main-track clips are split in the silence before and after the sentence, midway into it and at most 0.15 s from the words. When only silence lies between that point and a cut, or the cut is less than 0.3 s away, the zoom reaches the cut instead, so every piece of a take keeps words and its pauses can still be removed. The pieces in between scale up by the factor, keeping their position and rotation. The sound does not change. Clips with keyframes keep their own motion, and the toast says how many did. The suggestions go when the speech changes. Suggest zooms is disabled with the reason while clips are not transcribed or recognition runs, Apply while clips are not transcribed, and both, like every editing control, while the AI is editing.
- **Keep in place while cutting**: one checkbox per audio track, saved with the project and undoable. Tracks made for music and sound files start checked and keep playing across cuts; sound detached from a video starts unchecked, so it is cut with the picture. The setting also applies to `Q`/`W`.

### Export
`Ctrl+E` or Export opens a dialog: preset, resolution (720p, 1080p, 1440p, 4K, with the resulting pixel size for the canvas), frame rate (24–60, default the project's), quality (High, Recommended, Smaller file) with an estimated file size. Preset opens a menu: Custom, then the presets that fit the canvas, then the others, disabled, each with what it needs ("Needs 9:16") and the full reason in its tooltip. The presets are Reels & TikTok (9:16, 1080×1920, 30 fps), YouTube Shorts (9:16, 1080×1920), YouTube 1080p and YouTube 4K (16:9, 1920×1080 and 3840×2160), Instagram feed (4:5, 1080×1350, 30 fps) and Square (1:1, 1080×1080, 30 fps); fitting ones show their size and fixed frame rate in the menu. Picking one sets its resolution, its frame rate (the YouTube presets start from the project's, as YouTube wants the rate the video was shot at) and Recommended, and the dialog says "Loudness −14 LUFS, applied to the file": the export writes H.264 High with AAC 48 kHz stereo and levels the sound to −14 LUFS integrated with true peak at most −1 dBTP (ITU-R BS.1770), while the project and the preview keep their own levels. Quality can change within any preset, and the frame rate within the YouTube ones (up to 60); another resolution, or another frame rate where the preset fixes 30, switches back to Custom. When the canvas changes to a format the chosen preset does not fit, the preset says to switch Ratio to its format and Export… is disabled with that reason; no hidden crop or stretch. Preset, resolution and quality are remembered; a remembered preset comes back only on a project it fits. A preset export first measures the loudness (the dialog's progress says "Measuring loudness" for the first few percent), then renders; Cancel stops either phase within a second. Export… asks where to save and adds `.mp4` when the name has no extension. An existing file is only replaced once someone said so: the save dialog asks for the name it returns, and when the final name differs (`.mp4` was added) or the file appears while rendering, the dialog asks “<name> already exists. Replace it?” with Choose another name… and Replace. Tab stays inside the dialog. It can be closed at any time, with `Esc` too unless an export is running (then Keep editing or Cancel export says what happens to it), and focus returns to what opened it; a running export shows its percentage in the top bar and clicking it reopens the dialog. Progress shows percentage and time left, with Cancel. When CC BY sounds from the sound library play in the video, export also writes their credits beside it as `<name>.credits.txt` (`reel.mp4` gets `reel.credits.txt`), and an existing credits file is replaced only once someone said so for that file: the dialog asks “reel.credits.txt already exists. Replace it?” on its own, since replacing the video is not consent to replace notes kept under that name. Success offers Show in folder, in the dialog or, when it is closed, in a toast. Failure shows the reason and Retry (same file); with the dialog closed a toast offers Details. Export is disabled with a reason while the timeline is empty.

Under the title, Video, Cover and Recap choose what to export; the dialog opens on Cover in the cover editor (`Ctrl+E` there too). Cover offers the format (9:16 Cover or 16:9 YouTube, with its size) and the file: PNG, or JPEG at the best quality under 2 MB, YouTube's limit. PNG is the default for the cover and JPEG for the YouTube thumbnail; the choice is remembered per format. Export… asks where to save in Pictures, named after the project ("Morning routine cover.png", "Morning routine YouTube thumbnail.jpg"), adds the extension when missing, and replaces an existing file only as the video does: the save dialog asks, and when the final name differs or the file appears meanwhile, the dialog asks “<name> already exists. Replace it?”. The export is a job (Rendering, Finding the person when the cover needs it) with Cancel; it writes through a temporary file, so a cancelled or failed export leaves nothing behind. A format without a cover says so and offers Make the cover, which opens the cover editor on it. Success offers Show in folder; with the dialog closed a toast reports the result.

Recap makes a short vertical video of how the video was cut, to show it off. It opens on the final numbers over the dimmed first frame. Then the recording plays fast: every cut passage is grey under a red edge with a label (Retake, 4 words, Pause 1.2 s), the transcript runs as captions whose cut words are struck through in red as the recording passes them, a counter falls from the length before to the length after while the words, retakes and pauses removed add up as each cut passes, and a bar shows the whole recording with the cuts in red and a playhead. It ends with the first 8 s of the finished cut, labelled After with its length for 4 s, with its own sound and its captions, then a 1.5 s card “Cut in Nuzky · nuzky.app”, which End card turns off (remembered). The fast part is silent but for a whoosh into it and a tick as each cut passes. The recording plays at least 4× its speed and takes 3–17 s, so a recap is at most 30 s; above 8× it shows ten pictures a second. A recap is 1080 × 1920 at 30 fps, made on this computer from the project, its versions and its transcripts, and nothing is sent anywhere. Videos exported as Video or Cover never carry Nuzky's name.

Shows chooses what the recap covers. Everything cut since import compares the project with the recording as imported: every video the main track plays now or played in a kept version, whole, in the order they were imported. An AI run compares the project before and after that run, as its run card does; Shows lists the eight newest runs kept in Versions. Result gives the numbers the recap will show, the same as the run card's: the lengths, then the words, retakes and pauses removed, leaving out the ones that are none. A retake is an attempt at a sentence that was said again, removed while another attempt stayed, an attempt the speaker gave up on, or a whole take dropped from the main track; a pause is a cut without a word. When a file that was cut has no transcript, the recap shows no words, retakes or pauses, and the dialog names the file. While nothing was cut, Result says so and Export… is disabled with the reason; so it is when a run's versions are no longer kept. Saving, replacing, progress, Cancel and Show in folder work as for the video; the name starts as "<project> recap.mp4". Recaps run one at a time with exports.

### Covers
A 9:16 cover for Reels and TikTok (1080 × 1920) and a YouTube thumbnail (1280 × 720), one of each per project, made from a frame of the video with text that can go behind the person. The Cover tile at the start of the main track, as in CapCut, opens the cover editor: it shows the 9:16 cover as it exports (else the YouTube one), or an empty shape until there is one, and is disabled with the reason while the timeline is empty. While the editor is open the top bar says so: Back to video in place of Projects, "Cover" before the project's name, and Export cover in place of Export. Back to video, clicking the tile again or `Esc` (the first `Esc` clears what is selected on the cover) closes the editor; the playhead and the clip selection come back as they were.

- **The editor**: the preview shows the cover in place of the video, drawn by the engine exactly as it exports, and the transport becomes the cover bar: the frame's time, 9:16 Cover or 16:9 YouTube, and what the apps cover. The library's place, titled Frames, holds Make it with AI until either format has a cover, Pick for me and the frames it found (the seven tabs act on the video, so they rest), the inspector the cover's settings, and the timeline chooses the frame: clips cannot be selected, moved or trimmed there, and nothing plays. Switching the format only changes what is shown.
- **The frame**: the playhead is the cover's frame. Clicking or dragging on the timeline, `←`/`→` (`Shift`: a second), `Home` and `End` choose it, one undo step per gesture or burst of keys; a frame changed by Undo, a candidate or an agent moves the playhead there. The frame time follows cuts as the engine keeps it on the same picture; a frame cut out of the video says so and asks for another.
- **Pick for me**: a job that looks through the whole video (Looking through the video, then Scoring frames) and cuts the person out of the best frame (Finding the person), with its phase, percentage and Stop. The first time it downloads the cover models (196 MB) in the same job, as the line under the buttons says; Stop works in every phase. It sets the cover to the best frame as one undo step, making the cover when there is none; a frame chosen while it ran is kept and a line says so. The frames it found (6 to 8, fewer in a short video, at least 2 s apart, best first) show as tiles with their time and why in plain words on one line ("Sharp, well framed, facing you"), the whole reason and their score out of 100 in the tooltip, and as dots on the ruler with the same tooltip. Frames that score under 40 % of the best, such as a blink beside open eyes, are left out. They serve both formats; Pick for me, then Pick again, scores the framing of the format shown. Before the first pick a line under it says what it finds; the timeline's own line says that clicking it chooses any frame. The grid is one Tab stop: the arrows move, `Enter` or `Space` chooses, which sets the frame and moves the playhead. Without a face in the video a line says the frames are the sharpest and best lit instead. Where faces cannot be found on this computer, Pick for me is disabled with the reason; choosing a frame and text in front still work.
- **Before there is a cover**: the editor shows the cover it would make at the playhead; nothing is saved until the first change (a frame, a text, a setting) or Export cover, which makes it as one undo step. A format without a cover, while the other has one, starts from that one: the editor opens on its frame and shows its outline and texts, their sizes scaled to the format. ### Reels from a long video
A long recording (a podcast, a talk, a stream) becomes several reels: the AI finds the moments that work on their own, the user picks and trims them, and each becomes a 9:16 project of its own. The long video itself never changes. It opens from Reels from a long video… in the home screen's New project menu, which asks for one video, makes a project of it and opens the mode with the keyboard on Find reels, and from Reels… in the Transcript tab of a project 3 minutes or longer (or one that already has reels). Done in the preview's bar or `Esc` goes back to editing, with the playhead and the clip selection as they were; opening another project leaves the mode.

- **The screen**: the panes keep their places, as in the cover editor. The reels take the timeline's place as cards, best first; the library's place shows the words of the reel chosen, to trim them; the inspector gives the preview its room, and the preview's bar plays the reel. The seven tabs and Export rest, as the mode changes no clips.
- **Find reels** opens the AI panel and sends it a prepared message: find the moments that work on their own and propose them for the user to pick, transcribing first when needed; the agent proposes them with `propose_reels`. While its agent answers, the list says the AI is finding reels; the cards arrive live as it proposes them, then the list says Find again, which replaces the reels not made yet. Without Claude Code or Codex on the computer and without ChatGPT signed in with its plan allowed, the list asks to sign in, with ChatGPT account…, Check again and Connect an agent in a terminal…; such an agent finds reels when asked to. When the agent found none, the list says so and how to ask it; when it failed, the AI panel says why.
- **A card**: the title, the hook (the sentence the reel opens with), its length once cut, why it works and its score, and the reel's cover once something makes one, an empty vertical shape until then. A reel over 60 s shows its length with a warning; it can still be made. Clicking a card, or the arrows on the focused list (`Home`, `End`), shows its reel; `Space` plays it, `Enter` checks or unchecks it (restores a rejected one, opens a made one), `Delete` rejects or restores it, `Ctrl+A` checks every proposed one. Its checkbox and Reject or Restore do the same by pointer. A rejected reel stays in its place, dimmed. Each change is one undo step.
- **Playing**: choosing a reel moves the playhead to its start. Play runs it to its end and stops there; Play again starts it over. The bar shows the playhead, Reel start (`Home`) and Hear the ending (`End`), which plays its last 3 s; the reel's span in the recording stands over its words. The preview shows the recording itself, so pauses play at their full length; with Center crop, the parts of the picture the crop leaves out are dimmed.
- **Trimming**: the words of the reel are marked in the text, with up to a minute of what is said around them, never reaching into another reel not made yet. A click plays from a word; `Shift`-click moves the nearer end of the reel to it: before the reel or in its first half the start, else the end. In the focused text `←`/`→` go word by word, `Alt+←`/`Alt+→` move the start and `Shift+←`/`Shift+→` the end by a word, and the playhead goes to the new start or 3 s before the new end. The words change at once and the length once cut follows as soon as Nuzky has measured it; a burst of keys is one undo step. A reel can start and end on any word. A made reel keeps its words. When a cut made since moved the reel's words, the text says so instead, and its card says so in place of why it works: it cannot be checked or made, and Find again proposes it anew. While the AI edits, the line under the words says why trimming waits.
- **Making**: the 9:16 choice is Center crop (the middle of the picture fills the frame) or Blurred background (the whole picture over a blurred copy), remembered. Make N reels makes the checked ones as a job: “Making reel 2 of 3 · 66%” with Stop in place of the button, which stops it within a moment and leaves nothing behind; opening another project stops it too. Each reel becomes a project beside the long video with only its words, pauses shortened as cuts shorten them, in the long video's collection; one undo step marks them made in the long video, and a toast says how many. A made card says so with Open and Export…, which opens the reel's project with the Export dialog on Reels & TikTok; a toast there offers Back to reels, which opens the long video in this mode again. When making fails, a line under the list says why and what to do (“Couldn't make the reels: …”), and nothing is made; Make tries again. When media files of the long video are missing, a line says to put them back, and Make waits with that reason.
- **While the AI edits**, checking, rejecting, trimming, the 9:16 choice, Make and Find reels are disabled with the reason; choosing reels, playing, Open and Export… still work.

### Connect your agent
"Connect an agent outside Nuzky…" in the AI panel's agent menu opens a dialog with Agent access, Claude Code and Codex, and copies for other agents.

- **Agent access**: a switch that decides whether agents outside Nuzky attached to the open project may edit it (those set up by Connect or a copy, which run `mcp --current`). Turning it off lets go of those agents at once; a run of theirs still open ends kept, as Stop does. While an AI run is open the switch and the checkbox are locked with the reason, so stopping the AI stays the top bar's Stop. A draft opened before says In Nuzky under its name and opens that project again; once it was saved in CapCut after the project last changed, it says Changed since instead. Its menu (right-click, Menu key or `Shift+F10`) offers Open “<project>” and Open as a new project, which brings the draft over again beside the project made before. An encrypted draft, as JianYing writes them, says in an error toast that it cannot be opened and to start a project from its exported video instead. The folder-plus button, or From a CapCut draft… in the New project menu, takes a folder: one draft opens at once, a folder of drafts kept elsewhere joins the cards and stays there on later starts, also in Learn my style; a folder without drafts says so in a toast.
- **Search** (`Ctrl+K`, `Ctrl+F`, or just start typing): names and collection names match at once, ignoring case and accents. Below them, Said in your videos lists where the words were said in transcribed projects of the collection shown, as a quote with the words highlighted and the time; clicking one opens the project with the playhead on the first word and the Transcript tab showing. Projects whose speech is not transcribed are named, so it is clear they were searched by name only; with nothing transcribed yet, it says so instead of saying nothing was found. `Enter` or `↓` moves to the first result, where `Enter` opens it; `Esc` clears the search.
- **Collections**: the sidebar shows All projects and each collection with its count. + (or New collection) names one in place and shows it, empty, with how to fill it. Drag cards onto a collection, or use Move to collection in a card's menu or the selection bar, to put projects there; a project is in at most one collection, and a toast offers Undo. A collection's menu (right-click, Menu key) renames it (`F2`) or deletes it; its projects stay, and a toast offers Undo.
- **Get set up**: the sidebar row under Your style opens Get set up over the home screen. Its count, in `accent`, is how many steps are still to do; with none left it shows no count.
- **Card menu** (right-click, the … button, Menu key or `Shift+F10`): Open, Rename (`F2`, in place on the card), Duplicate (`Ctrl+D`; the copy joins the same collection and gets focus), Move to collection, Show in folder, Move to Trash (`Delete`). While the menu is open, its card is outlined. The open project cannot go to the Trash; the reason shows.
- **Trash**: Move to Trash hides the project at once and says “Your videos stay where they are.” with Undo. Half a minute after that move, or when the window closes, the file goes to the system Trash, from where it can be put back; moving it again after Undo starts the half minute again. If another window or an agent opened it meanwhile, it stays where it is, shows again, and an error toast says why; on closing, the window stays open to show that toast, and closing again quits.
- **Undo**: `Ctrl+Z` takes back the last Move to Trash, Move to collection or deleted collection while its toast still offers Undo.
- **Selection**: `Ctrl`/`Shift`+click, `Space` on a focused card, or the round check in a card's corner selects; while anything is selected, a click selects too. A bar at the bottom moves the selection to a collection or the Trash; `Esc` or ✕ clears it. `Ctrl+A` selects every card shown.
- **Keyboard**: the cards are one Tab stop; arrow keys move by where the cards sit, `Home`/`End` to the first and last.
- **Updates**: the foot of the sidebar says which Nuzky this is, or “Update available” with the newer version. Its menu offers Download Nuzky <version>… (the release page in the browser, where the installers are), Check for updates and Check automatically (on by default). The automatic check runs 10 s after start and at most once a day, also across launches; it says nothing when it fails. In the editor, while a newer version is out, a small button with the arrow next to Projects opens the same menu; nothing else appears over the editor. Check for updates always answers in a toast: the newer version with Download, that this is the latest version, or that the check failed and what to try. The check reads only the version number from the latest release on GitHub; Nuzky downloads and installs nothing by itself. With `NUZKY_NO_UPDATE_CHECK=1` it never checks and the foot shows only the version.
- **Send feedback**: above the version at the foot of the sidebar, and as a round button next to Projects in the editor. Its menu offers Report a bug… and Share an idea…, which open the repository's bug report or feature request form on GitHub in the browser with this Nuzky's version (and for a bug its system and architecture) already filled in; Report a bug… also copies the diagnostics, and a toast says to paste them into the report. Copy diagnostics puts this Nuzky's version, system, graphics and the last 200 lines of its log on the clipboard. Open logs folder shows the `logs` folder in Nuzky's data folder in the file manager. Message me on X… opens the author's profile. Nothing leaves Nuzky by itself: the person reads and sends the report on GitHub, and no video or transcript goes with it.

### Your style
Your style in the home screen's sidebar, under All projects, shows the style the AI follows when it edits: the file `EDIT.md`, which every agent connected to Nuzky reads as it is. Its count, in `accent`, is how many suggestions wait; without any it shows no count. Nothing of it leaves this computer.

- **Suggestions**: what learning found that is new or would change a rule of the style, each with what it says, the change (“Longest pause: 120 ms → 400 ms”), the confidence (High in 3 or more videos, Medium in 2, Low in 1) with in how many of the videos learned from and at how many moments it holds, and Show moments: each moment as the video, the time and what happened there (four, then Show all). Accept puts the rule into the style, Reject leaves it out; with more than one, Accept all takes every one. A suggestion changes nothing until it is accepted, and the same rule learned again with numbers within 15 % is no news.
- **Talk it through with AI**: AI in the top bar (`Ctrl+J`), or Talk it through with AI above Your rules, opens the AI panel beside the page; Close or `Ctrl+J` puts it away. It is the same agent and conversation as in the editor, but what is sent from here is about the style, not the open video (the field shows Your style instead of the clip and playhead chips). The agent reads the style, talks the change through, and once you agree makes it: your words become one of Your rules, or it changes, accepts or rejects a rule. The page shows the change at once, without reloading: the changed rule lights up for a moment and a toast says “The AI changed your style: …” with Undo. Every change it makes is a version starting with AI:. Only you can go back to the default style.
- **Your rules**: “Tell the AI what to do or not to do” takes a rule in plain words; `Enter` adds it. A rule's row changes it in place (the pencil, `F2` or `Enter` on the focused row; `Enter` saves, `Esc` cancels) or removes it (the bin or `Delete`, with Undo in the toast). They stand in the style under Your rules, before everything learned, and the agent is told they win over everything else. Learning never changes them.
- **Learned rules**: each rule in the style with what it tells the AI and how well the videos learned from back it now (or Not seen lately). A rule changed by hand says Changed by you, and learning leaves it as it is. Its … menu Reverts it to how the style had it before its last change or Removes it, both with Undo in a toast.
- **Not learned** (closed at first): rules the creator rejected, removed or reverted, which learning leaves alone, each with Learn again.
- **Learned from**: the videos the suggestions come from, newest first: a recording and the finished video cut from it with their match, or a project with when it was learned. The newest 12 count.
- **Learn my style…** opens a dialog with three tabs; the arrow keys move between them. **Videos** takes 1 to 3 pairs of a recording and the finished video cut from it (Add a pair, the ✕ removes one). Learn compares them one after another on this computer the way `nuzky style learn` does, so the same files give the same rules: each row shows its phase and percentage, then Matched with the share of the finished video's speech found in the recording, or why it was skipped (a finished video not cut from its recording). Review shows the suggestions. **Projects (.otio)** takes timelines cut in another editor and exported as OpenTimelineIO, which DaVinci Resolve and other editors write: Choose projects… (any number, Add projects for more, the ✕ removes one) reads each at once and says what it will teach (each recording whose sound it plays, with its clips, the length and the titles) and what is missing: a recording not on this computer, a sound file, and what the file holds that Nuzky does not read (effects, nested sequences, media on the web, which is never fetched). Learn recognises the speech of each recording the way `nuzky style learn --from` does; each row shows its phase over a progress bar, with the percentage once it is known, then Learned or why nothing was. **CapCut** lists the drafts it finds where CapCut keeps them (`~/Movies/CapCut/User Data/Projects/com.lveditor.draft` on macOS, `%LOCALAPPDATA%\CapCut\User Data\Projects\com.lveditor.draft` on Windows), newest first, each with its name, when it was last changed and its length; empty drafts are left out. Ticking a draft (its checkbox carries the draft's name, so `Space` ticks it from the keyboard) ticks it at once, says Reading… and reads it and says what it will teach and what is missing, as a project does; an encrypted draft, as JianYing writes them, says it cannot be read and to learn from the finished video instead. Add a draft folder… takes one draft's folder, which it lists and ticks, or a folder of drafts kept elsewhere, which joins the list unticked and stays on later starts, also on the home screen; a folder without drafts says so in a toast. Nuzky reads the draft's timeline itself (the cut, speed, volume, framing and zoom keyframes, captions with their look and place, and transitions), so the captions' look and the zoom come from the draft rather than from pixels; it finds media copied into the draft's folder too, and never fetches anything. Learn then works as it does for projects. When it ends, the dialog shows `EDIT.md` as learned, with Your rules kept. Nothing changes until Use as my style writes it and keeps what was learned; Learn from more goes back to the projects or drafts. Over an existing style the button is Replace my style…, which asks first, focused on Cancel (`Esc` answers it too): the learned rules, also those changed by hand, give way, Your rules stay. Either way a toast offers Undo and Versions keep the style before. When the style changed since the preview, anywhere, it is not replaced: a toast says so and Learn shows what replacing it would do now. While AI is editing, the style cannot be replaced and the button says why. In every tab the dialog can close; the learning goes on in the top bar, which opens it again, and a toast reports the result. Stop ends it within a second.
- **Edit as text** shows the whole `EDIT.md` to read and change; `Ctrl+S` or Save keeps it as written, Cancel (or `Esc` while unchanged) leaves it. A rule changed or deleted there is left alone by learning from then on. If the style changed in another window meanwhile, saving says so and keeps the text.
- **Live**: a change made anywhere else, by an agent in a terminal, `nuzky style learn`, another window or a text editor, shows on the page within half a second, the changed rules lit up for a moment.
- **Learning by itself**: after every export (from the app or by an agent), and 10 s after you change a project an AI cut and its run was kept (or as soon as the project is switched or closed), Nuzky reads the project and the suggestions update: what you kept, cut and shortened, your captions and zoom, and the words you corrected, which become Spelling. It never learns from the AI's cut as the AI left it, nor from edits that undo the AI's run, and it changes nothing in the style until you accept. When that brings new suggestions, a toast says “Learned from <project>: 2 new suggestions” with Review.
- **Versions** (the clock): every change of the style, newest first: accepting, rejecting, removing, reverting, your rules, editing by hand, a change made to the file outside Nuzky, learning with `nuzky style learn` or from projects of another editor, and restores. Choosing one restores it as a new version, with Undo in a toast. Back to default… asks first, then removes the style, so the AI follows Nuzky's defaults, and forgets what was learned from your videos; a toast offers Undo, and Versions bring the style back.

### Get set up
Get set up keeps the steps that set Nuzky up in one dialog: Captions model and Connect your AI, in that order. Get set up on the first start and the Get set up row in the home screen's sidebar open it over the home screen. A step shows once it works; later updates add more.

- **Steps**: the rail on the left lists the steps, each with its number, or a check once it is done, and Done or Skipped under its title. The header counts the done steps ("1 of 2 done"); a skipped step does not count. Clicking a step shows it. A step taller than the dialog scrolls inside it, between its title and Skip, Next or Done.
- **Skip, Next and Done**: a step still to do offers Skip, which marks it Skipped and shows the next step; on the last step it stays there. A done or skipped step offers Next, which shows the next step, and on the last step Done, which closes the dialog. A step is done when its own work is finished there.
- **Captions model**: picks the spoken language and the model Captions and Transcript use, and downloads ahead of time everything the first transcript in that language needs, so the first captions download nothing. The spoken language is the one Captions uses: the computer's language when it is offered, else Detect automatically, and a change here is a change there. Under it, Fast, Balanced and Most accurate, each with its size, Downloaded or Not downloaded, and how many words it gets wrong in that language as published for Whisper; Fast and Balanced also say how many times quicker than Most accurate they are on this kind of computer, as measured on a Mac with the graphics card and with the processor. Recommended marks Most accurate when speech is recognised on the graphics card (Metal on macOS, Vulkan on Linux), and a smaller model when it is recognised on the processor (Windows): Fast where it gets fewer than 1 word in 10 wrong in the language, else Balanced; a line says which. Until a model was picked anywhere, the step starts on the recommended one. In Czech and English the download includes the language's word timing model, and a line says how big it is; in another language a line says word times will be estimates; with Detect automatically it says which languages get measured word times. Download <size> in the footer, before Skip, downloads the model, the voice detector and the word timing model as one job: the step shows what it is downloading with the percentage and Stop, and so does the top bar. Stop ends it within a second or two, and Resume download goes on where it stopped. A failed download says why, with Open logs folder, and Try again goes on from there; while Get set up is open no toast repeats it. Before it starts, Download checks the free space where Nuzky keeps its models; when it is short, the step says how much more it needs and downloads nothing. While it downloads, the language and the model wait. When Download, Stop or Try again goes away, the focus moves to what took its place. The step is done once the model picked is downloaded, also when it already was on opening, and when a download started here ends while the step was skipped or Get set up closed. While an agent edits, the language, the models and Download are locked with the reason; Stop is not.
- **Connect your AI**: a row each for Claude Code and Codex: the name with the version once installed, and one line with how far it is: Not installed, Not signed in, Signed in, Connecting…, Connected or Couldn't connect. Nuzky finds them where their installers put them, also when it was not started from a terminal, asks each for its own sign-in (`claude auth status`, `codex login status`) and reads its settings. A missing one shows its official install command for macOS and Linux (`curl -fsSL https://claude.ai/install.sh | bash`, `curl -fsSL https://chatgpt.com/codex/install.sh | sh`) with Copy, which shows a check for 1.5 s, and Install guide, which opens the agent's guide in the browser; Nuzky installs nothing. A signed-out one shows what to run to sign in (`claude`, `codex login`). Check again in the footer looks again, and finds an agent installed meanwhile. Connect does what Connect your agent does (the `nuzky` entry after a backup, Agent access on, also at every launch), then checks that the agent really reaches this Nuzky: Claude Code runs its own `claude mcp get nuzky`, which starts the server; for Codex, Nuzky reads the entry with `codex mcp get nuzky` and starts it the way Codex starts its servers, with only the variables Codex hands them. The check passes only when that server hands this app a one-time code over its private socket and Agent access is on; Claude Code gets 20 seconds, Codex 10 to answer and its server 15 to reach Nuzky. Rows under the line say what Connect did (Backed up, Added Nuzky and turned on Agent access) and what the check does, with its seconds. A failed check says why and what to do, and Connect turns into Try again, which connects and checks again. Connected says to restart the agent if it is open. An agent connected before is checked again whenever the step shows, so Connected always means it reaches Nuzky now. The step is done once one agent is connected. A settings file Nuzky cannot read is left alone, and the row says to fix it and check again. While one agent connects, the other's Connect waits with the reason.
- **Without AI**: skipped, and on Windows, where the step first says that AI editing isn't available there yet, Connect your AI shows how to cut without AI: open a talking head video, Transcribe timeline in Transcript, Remove retakes and pauses, then select other words and press Delete. Open a video… in the footer asks for one video; once one is picked, Get set up closes and the editor opens on the video's Transcript tab. Set up Claude Code or Codex instead brings the agents back.
- **Remembered**: the steps' states and the step shown stay when the dialog closes and when Nuzky quits, so it opens again where it was left.
- **Keyboard**: the rail is one Tab stop: `↑`/`↓` show the previous or next step, `Home`/`End` the first or last. Opening puts the focus on the step shown, never on Skip or Download, so a stray `Enter` or `Space` skips or downloads nothing. In Connect your AI, Tab goes through each row's Install guide, Copy and Connect, then the footer; when Connect goes away the focus moves to Done, Try again keeps it where Connect was, and Skip leaves it on Open a video…. `Esc`, ×, Done or a click beside it close it, and the focus goes back to what opened it. While it is open, the editor's and the home screen's keys wait.

### Launcher
Projects in the editor's top bar, or `Ctrl+K`, opens the launcher over the editor: a search field, Continue (the open project, with the AI run when there is one), the five most recent other projects, and All projects, which goes to the home screen. Below, Empty project starts an empty project in 9:16, 16:9, 1:1 or 4:5, From videos… one of videos, and Open file… opens one from anywhere. It searches like the home screen: projects by name, then Said in your videos, six of each; when there are more, Show all results continues the search on the home screen. The keyboard stays in the search field: `↑`/`↓` move, `Enter` opens (the first result once you type), `Esc` closes and keeps editing; `Tab` reaches the buttons. While an agent edits, the question about its changes shows inside the launcher; `Esc` there cancels it.

### Errors
An error says in plain words what happened and what to do next; an error toast stays until it is dismissed. The engine's coded errors (`READ_ONLY: …`, `EDIT_REJECTED: …` and the like) are translated where they are shown; an error without a known code is shown as it came.

A job that fails ends with a way on. A failed export shows its reason in the Export dialog with Open logs folder and Retry (Export… when the dialog did not start it); with the dialog closed, a toast offers Details, which opens it. A failed transcript or captions run, also when its speech model could not be downloaded, shows in its panel with Open logs folder under the button that starts it again, and its toast offers Try again, which runs the same request. A failed person model download offers Try again. A job that hit a bug in Nuzky says so and asks to try again and report it if it happens again.

Nuzky keeps a log in the `logs` folder of its data folder: warnings, errors, failed jobs, errors of the interface and panics, with where they happened. It holds two files of up to 1 MB each (`nuzky.log` and the older `nuzky.1.log`). It never keeps what someone made or who they are: text between double quotes, curly quotes or backticks, where captions, typed words and project names appear in error messages, is left out, a path keeps only its last part, and tokens, keys, `Authorization` headers and long hexadecimal secrets are hidden. Debug and trace records, where libraries dump whole requests, stay in the terminal. Nothing is ever sent; the person copies the diagnostics or opens the folder.

When the window itself hits a bug, it shows "Something went wrong in this window" with Report a bug… and Reload (focused) instead of an empty window; the card itself says whether the diagnostics were copied. The project lives in the engine, so Reload continues where it was.

When Nuzky ended without quitting, because it crashed, hung and was killed or the computer lost power, the next start says "Nuzky closed unexpectedly. Your project is as it was last saved." with Report a bug…, after the project opened as described in Starting; an unfinished AI edit still asks what to keep (Interrupted AI edits). Quitting is not a crash, and on Linux and macOS neither is logging out, closing the terminal that runs Nuzky or `kill`. Each running Nuzky holds a lock on its own file in the logs folder, so another open window is never taken for a crash.

### Saving
Every edit is saved about a second later. The top bar shows Saving…, Saved, or a failure with an icon and text. An action that changes nothing, such as a failed import, leaves the status as it was.

## Interrupted AI edits

Opening a project with an unfinished AI checkpoint blocks the editor with “An AI edit didn't finish”. “Keep changes” keeps the saved edits. “Restore previous version” restores the checkpoint as one undoable step and offers Undo in a toast. The dialog cannot be dismissed before choosing; a failed recovery keeps it open with the error. While an AI run is open, the top bar shows “AI is editing · <label>” with Stop and edit, and the editing controls are disabled: the inspector, the tools in the left panel, the timeline's edit buttons, track toggles and new transitions, moving and trimming clips, the preview box handles, Ratio and renaming; in the cover editor Pick for me, the frames, the cover's settings and its handles, and choosing a frame on the timeline, which goes back to the agent's frame. Disabled buttons stay reachable by keyboard and give that reason; fields are disabled with it as their tooltip. Selecting, playing and scrubbing still work. An edit tried anyway, by shortcut or drop, is not sent and shows “AI is editing. Stop it to edit yourself.” with Stop and edit. That toast stays until the run ends; dismissed, it comes back with the next refused edit. When a run that changed something ends, “AI edit done” offers Undo for the whole run; for a run of the AI panel's agent, the panel's run card does that instead.
