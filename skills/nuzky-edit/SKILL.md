# Edit with Nuzky

Use MCP for every project change. Never write project JSON or overwrite source media. Media, recognition and export stay local; transcripts and inspected frames are visible to your model.

## Cut slips and pauses, then caption

1. `get_state`: check the canvas, assets, duration, speech_layout_key and any open run. If recovery_checkpoint is present, ask whether to keep current edits or restore, then `resolve_recovery` with that choice.
2. `transcribe` with no arguments recognises all heard files lacking stored words. Language defaults to auto and model to the best installed. Wait for it with `job` action `wait`, calling it again while it runs; the first Czech or English file also downloads the word timing model. When no speech model is installed, Nuzky asks the user whether to download it while the job waits (phase `waiting_for_user`, then `downloading_speech_model`); keep waiting, it goes on by itself. MODEL_DECLINED means the user said not now or stopped the download: do not call transcribe again, tell them what you can do without a transcript, such as cutting long silences with `analyze(kind: "silences")`, and that the Transcript tab downloads the model. `word_times: "measured"` means cuts land on word boundaries even in speech without pauses; with `"estimated"` (no model for the language, or offline) check cuts in connected speech with get_waveform before keeping them. Explicit asset_ids force recognition again; avoid this unless needed. Transcribe before opening a run.
3. `get_transcript`: read ALL sentences before choosing cuts. For long videos use `detail: "sentences"` with `limit` and pass `next_cursor` as `cursor` until null, keeping the same `range_us` on each page. Request full word-level detail only for the part you cut. Words have zero-based `i`, timeline start_us/end_us, text and probability `p`. Sentences have inclusive `from`/`to` word indices; pauses show after_word and gap_us. Optional range_us filters without renumbering. Transcribe any untranscribed assets before continuing.
4. Start every rough cut with `analyze(kind: "retakes")` right after reading the transcript. It needs no asset_id, answers at once and numbers words exactly as get_transcript (same transcript_key). `groups` are attempts at one restarted sentence, also across clips, with `keep` pointing at the last complete attempt; `fillers` are filler words: hesitation sounds recognition wrote ("ehm") and words such as "jakoby" or "like" set apart by a comma or a pause, at the start of a sentence or, set apart on both sides, inside one; only in Czech and English; `unfinished` are attempts that trail off before a new sentence; `review` lists near-repeats that differ in a negation, a number or a content word, which it never deletes; `suggested_delete` merges every group's `delete` with the fillers and unfinished attempts. Check each group and filler against the text: change a choice only when the text clearly shows another attempt is the one to keep, and decide each `review` pair yourself. Then mark what the analysis cannot know: slips, other false starts and fillers, off-topic detours. Keep the LAST complete take when a sentence restarts. Preserve negations, the argument and its final point. Remove closing calls to action such as “napiš mi do komentáře…” unless the user asks for one. Do not invent a duration target or remove a whole topic just to reach one. Then `analyze(kind: "fillers")` without asset_id: it answers at once with the same `fillers` and `hesitations` {after_word,start_us,end_us}, voice between two words that recognition did not write, such as an "eee" (AUDIO_NOT_READY names a job preparing the sound: wait, then call again). Cut them with `delete_hesitations: [after_word,...]` in the same edit_transcript; one next to a very short word may be that word, missed by recognition, so look at it with get_waveform before cutting. With asset_id it reads one file's stored words instead, in source microseconds; dictionary matches need context.
5. Delete the reviewed suggested_delete plus your own ranges through `edit_transcript`. Plan with `edit_transcript(transcript_key, delete: [[from,to],...], delete_hesitations: [...], dry_run: true)`, which needs no open run; alternatively use keep ranges. Word ranges are INCLUSIVE. Use the key from get_transcript. The tool handles source mapping, tight cut padding, edge silence and internal pauses longer than shorten_pauses_us (default 300000). Cuts stay inside clips that carry words, so B-roll is never cut. Never calculate source-to-timeline offsets yourself. Check duration_us.before/after and preview_text. Then `begin_run` and apply with its run_id, dry_run=false, a fresh request_id and the ORIGINAL transcript_key, not the predicted key from the preview. On a save failure, retry identical arguments with that same request_id; the live cut may already be applied. Omitting request_id generates one, so supply it for retryable edits. SPEECH_CHANGED means read the transcript and plan again. Omit delete/keep to shorten pauses only. One rippleDeleteRanges edit respects music tracks kept in place.
6. Hear every cut in speech before you keep it. `get_waveform(range_us)` over the edited part, up to 60 s a call, lists its `cuts` with `t_us` and `split_words`; then look at each cut with `range_us: [max(0, t_us - 1000000), t_us + 1000000]`. The orange line is the cut, and shaded beyond it is the sound the cut threw away. Sound that runs from the line into the shade, or a red word, means the cut took part of a word: move that edge into the quiet beside the word in the same run, with one word more or fewer or a `trimClip`. Sound kept beside a line with no word over it is usually a breath or a noise: trim it when quiet separates it from the next word, and leave it when it runs into one, since word times can miss a syllable. A clean cut has quiet on both sides of the line.
7. `build_captions(run_id)` uses current derived words and creates or replaces one Captions track (a text track named `Captions`; titles stay on other text tracks). Default Reel style: size 95, white, regular, black stroke 7.5, no background, Inter; defaults max_words=2/max_chars=15. A single long word stays intact. Captions never span a clip cut; vertical canvases automatically use the safe wrap width. Prefer defaults unless asked otherwise. `style_preset` chooses a built-in style package (e.g. `"hormozi"`) or the user's saved style from My styles by name, with its look, font when it has one, entry/exit animations and key word colour. Key words are numbers and words said louder than the median word, scored from sound as analyze emphasis does; captions mark them when made, keeping them through regeneration and word corrections. When the user wants the spoken word highlighted (karaoke, "zvýrazni slovo"), pass `style_preset: "karaoke"` (Reel look, the word being said in yellow) or `"green_box"` (bold white on a dark box, the word in green); give style or style_preset, not both. Correcting a caption word for word (as many words as before) keeps its highlight; adding or removing words turns it off.
8. `inspect_frames(times_us: [...], safe_area: true)` around cuts, caption changes and representative phrases; `inspect_frames(sample: "changes", safe_area: true)` reviews the whole cut with one frame per distinct picture. Shaded margins are outside the IG/TikTok safe area. Check words, readable captions, the face and the ending. Correct transcription mistakes with `correct_words(run_id, transcript_key, request_id, corrections: [{i, text}])`, i from get_transcript and text with its punctuation, never by editing caption text: it fixes the word in the transcript and in the captions that show it, and build_captions keeps it. Never invent speech. After manual caption edits inspect get_state.caption_stats; keep multiword captions within chosen limits. Never abbreviate speech just to fit. Do not add zoom unless asked.
9. `end_run(run_id, action: "keep")`, then export with the preset of the platform: `export_video(path: "reel.mp4", preset: "reels")` for Instagram Reels or TikTok (1080x1920, 30 fps), `"shorts"` for YouTube Shorts (1080x1920), `"youtube_1080p"` or `"youtube_4k"` for a YouTube video (1920x1080, 3840x2160), `"instagram_feed"` for an Instagram post (1080x1350, 30 fps) and `"square"` for a square post (1080x1080, 30 fps). Each writes H.264 High and AAC 48 kHz with the sound levelled to -14 LUFS and true peak at most -1 dBTP in the file (the project keeps its levels). Each needs its canvas ratio (9:16, 16:9, 4:5, 1:1); never change the user's format just to fit a preset. Shorts and YouTube keep the project frame rate, or the fps you pass, up to 60. Other videos: `export_video(path, resolution, fps, quality)` as the user asks; the resolution is the short side. Wait for it with `job` action `wait` until done and report the real output path, duration and any uncertainty. The output path must be new.

## Finish a draft from CapCut

When get_state has `imported`, the project was opened from the creator's CapCut draft: the cut, captions, titles, zooms and music there are their own work. Asked to finish it ("dodělej tenhle draft", Finish this draft in my style), carry on from it; never start over or rebuild what is there.

1. Read get_state, `get_style` and `imported.left_out`, and say in a sentence what did not come over. Clips of a file that is missing or does not open keep their place without picture or sound: leave them where they are, name the files, and say that export needs them back or the clips replaced.
2. Transcribe and read the whole transcript as above. Cut only what the creator left uncut and the style says goes (slips, retakes, long pauses), with `edit_transcript` and a dry run first; the cuts already there stay, so the duration should change little.
3. Captions: keep a Captions track that covers the speech as it is; fix a wrong word with updateClip `text`, since these captions carry no words for correct_words. Only when there are none, or they stop partway, run `build_captions`: it replaces the whole track in the look of the captions there, so say their own wording goes.
4. Zoom and motion as the style asks, on clips without keyframes (apply_zooms and apply_motion leave keyed clips alone anyway).
5. Redo something that did not come over only when the style or the user wants it, and say what you used instead: `cleanVoice` for Noise reduction or Enhance voice, `normalize` for Normalize loudness, a Nuzky transition (`dissolve`, `fadeBlack`, `zoomIn`, `slideLeft`…) for one Nuzky does not have, `adjust` for colour adjustments.
6. Inspect frames with safe_area=true, end the run, and tell the user what you changed.

## See the footage

For a running job, use `job` action `wait` and call it again while it runs. Tell the user once what runs and roughly how long it takes from `remaining_s`. After that, write only when something changes. Never ask whether to keep waiting.

Find where something happens before looking at frames. `activity(range_us?, points?)` returns text only: per step of the timeline `change` (the biggest jump in the picture; 18 or more is a hard cut), `motion` (how much moves within 0.1 s) and `loudness_db` (the mix), and the `peaks` of each with their times. A reel answers at once; a long range may answer with a job: wait with job action wait until done or cancel it. Then look only where it matters: `inspect_frames(times_us)` at the change peaks, or `inspect_frames(sample: "changes", range_us)` over a range that interests you. It keeps only frames that look different from the frames kept before, so a talking head costs a frame per shot; pass `next` back as `cursor` while it is not null. Do not sample a talking head evenly by hand.

## Wide footage for a reel

When the footage is wide and the video goes to Reels, TikTok or Shorts, call `reframe(run_id, format: "9:16", clip_ids?)` inside a run and wait with `job` action `wait`, before `build_captions`, so the captions wrap inside the vertical safe area. It changes the canvas and places every main-track video and image clip (or only `clip_ids`) to follow the speaker's face, centring a clip without one; it keeps a punch-in zoom, replaces the moved clips' keyframes and undoes with the run. `"4:5"` and `"1:1"` do the same for the feed. When the face and person models are missing, the open Nuzky app asks the user while the job waits (phase `waiting_for_user`, then `downloading_vision_models`); keep waiting. MODEL_DECLINED means the user said not now or stopped it: do not call tools needing those models again, carry on without them and mention that Get set up downloads them. Without the app MODEL_MISSING says what to tell the user. Check the result with `inspect_frames(sample: "changes", safe_area: true)`.

## Pick a cover frame

For a cover, thumbnail or "náhledovka", run `analyze(kind: "thumbnail_frames")` after the cut: it looks at the picture as exported, with colours and zooms but without text, as a job; wait with job action wait. Pass `params: {format: "9:16"}` for a Reels or TikTok cover and `"16:9"` for a YouTube thumbnail; the canvas shape is the default. `candidates` (6-8, best first, at least 2 s apart) carry `parts` that say why: eyes_open, mouth (not mid-word), facing the camera, smile against this face's usual look, sharpness, exposure and framing for each format, plus `crops` for each format. Look at the top ones with `inspect_frames(times_us)` and choose; the score guides, it does not decide. To put text behind the person, `segment_subject(time_us)` returns the subject's mask as a grayscale PNG in the cache with its box and share of the frame; it takes several seconds and the same frame comes from the cache next time. When the face and person models are missing, the open Nuzky app asks the user while the job waits (phase `waiting_for_user`, then `downloading_vision_models`); keep waiting. MODEL_DECLINED means the user said not now or stopped it: do not call tools needing those models again, carry on without them and mention that Get set up downloads them. Without the app MODEL_MISSING says what to tell the user. VISION_UNAVAILABLE: covers cannot run on this computer; say so and carry on with the edit.

The project keeps one cover (`cover_9x16`, 1080×1920 for Reels, TikTok and Shorts) and one YouTube thumbnail (`youtube_16x9`, 1280×720); `get_state.thumbnails` shows them. The `thumbnail` prompt makes both. Inside a run, `apply_edits` `{"type":"setThumbnail","thumbnail":{format, timeUs, frame, background, texts, outline}}` replaces the one of its format and `removeThumbnail {format}` drops it. From the bottom up it draws the background, texts with `behind: true`, the person cut out of the frame at `timeUs` by its mask, the outline around them and the other texts. `frame` is a transform as for clips: scale 1 fits the whole frame inside the thumbnail, x and y move its centre by fractions of the thumbnail. `background` {picture, blur 0–1, dim 0–1, color}: picture true shows the frame behind the person, enlarged about its centre until it covers the thumbnail, false only the colour. `outline` {color, width up to 100 px}. Texts are {text, style, transform, behind} with sizes in thumbnail pixels; the text presets are in the `thumbnail` prompt. Text behind the person, an outline or a background that is blurred, dimmed or a colour need the mask of the person; inspect_thumbnail starts it as a job and says MASK_NOT_READY until it is made. For a cover from a vertical video keep the frame as it is and put big text behind the head; for YouTube from a vertical video put the person on a third with a blurred or coloured background and the hook beside them. `inspect_thumbnail(format, safe_zones: true)` draws it as the file will be, with the share of each text behind the person that they hide (`hidden`, 0.05–0.3 reads well), and tints what the Reels interface, the 3:4 profile grid and YouTube's duration badge cover. `export_thumbnail(format, path)` writes a new .png or .jpg as a job; use .jpg for YouTube, which stays under its 2 MB limit. Cuts made later move `timeUs` with the picture.

## Reels from a long video

When the user wants several reels from one long recording (a podcast, a talk, a stream), find the moments, let them choose, then make each a project of its own.

1. Transcribe as above, then read the whole video with `get_transcript(detail: "sentences", limit: 100)`, passing `next_cursor` back until it is null. Look for moments that stand alone: they open with a line that makes someone stay (a question, a bold claim, a surprise), keep to one idea, end on its payoff and need nothing said before them.
2. In a run, `propose_reels(transcript_key, candidates: [{from, to, title, why, score}])` with the best 3 to 10, each from the first word of a sentence to the last word of one and 15 to 60 s once cut. The tool measures each and refuses one that is too long, overlaps another or starts or ends mid-sentence; fix that one and propose again. It does not compare with reels already made, so read get_state's `reel_candidates` and do not propose their moments again. Write title and why in the user's language; why says in a sentence what makes it work alone. Proposing again replaces what was proposed before; made reels stay. Then `end_run` keep and ask the user which to make, or let them pick in the app.
3. In a run, `make_reels(ids)` makes the picked ones (status `picked` in get_state's `reel_candidates`), or the ones the user named. Each becomes a new project beside this one with only its words on a 9:16 canvas: `canvas: "crop"` (default) fills it with the middle of the picture, `"blur"` shows the whole picture over a blurred copy, better when the speaker is off centre or several people share the frame. The long video stays as it was. Report the paths.
4. Each reel is its own project, and this server edits only this one. Captions, a cover and the export happen in the reel, with the steps above, once the user opens it in Nuzky or through an MCP server started on its path (`nuzky mcp --project <path> --allow-write`).

## Make it dynamic, only when asked

For “rozhýbej to, občas zoom, pomalý nájezd”, or when the creator's style asks for zoom, read the story first. Keep it subtle, not on every sentence. For 1.15–1.3 punch-ins on key claims or punchlines run `analyze(kind: "emphasis")` after the cuts: it answers at once with zooms {from,to,start_us,end_us,text,score,scale} on get_transcript's word numbers and transcript_key, scored by loudness, exclamation and the opening hook, at least 5 s apart and about one per 12 s. AUDIO_NOT_READY names a job preparing the sound; wait with job action wait, then analyze again. Read each against the text: drop one that is not a claim or punchline, add a sentence it missed, keep the count low. Apply them inside a run with `apply_zooms(run_id, transcript_key, zooms: [{from,to,scale}], request_id)`: one undo step, the picture changes in the silence between words and the sound stays as it was; clips with keyframes are left alone and listed in skipped. For a slow push-in of 6–10 % over one sentence when building up, a pull-out at a reveal or turn, or a Ken Burns move on a photo, call `apply_motion(run_id, kind, strength, range_us or clip_id, request_id)` inside a run: kind `pushIn`, `pullOut` or `kenBurns`, strength 0.06, 0.1 or 0.15. `range_us` [start_us of the sentence's first word, end_us of its last] from get_transcript moves the main-track clips under it as one continuous move; `clip_id` moves one video or image clip, also on an overlay track, over its whole length or the part inside `range_us`. It writes two keyframes per clip on a smooth curve from the clip's own framing, centred on the middle of the safe area on vertical canvases so the face stays clear of the apps' buttons; nothing to compute. Clips that already have keyframes are left alone and listed in skipped. A constant base zoom around 1.1 is updateClip transform. Read nuzky://schema for exact edit fields. A complete transform is {"x":0,"y":0,"scale":1.2,"rotation":0,"opacity":1}. Inspect frames with safe_area=true after changes.

## B-roll and picture in picture

To show a screenshot, a second camera or other B-roll while the speaker says something, find the words in get_transcript and use the timeline start_us of the first word and end_us of the last. Inside a run, `apply_edits` with `{"type":"addPictureInPicture","assetId":…,"startUs":start,"durationUs":end-start}` puts the video or image in a small window with rounded corners and a soft shadow in the top right corner of the safe area, on an overlay track above the speaker; text cuts never cut it. For B-roll over the whole frame, follow with updateClip `transform` {"x":0,"y":0,"scale":1,"rotation":0,"opacity":1} and `shape` {}. updateClip `shape` sets `radius` (0–1 of half the shorter side), `borderWidth` (canvas px), `borderColor` and `shadow` (0–1); `transform.crop` {left,top,right,bottom} cuts those shares off the picture as shown, without moving the rest, and keyframes animate it. For a round camera bubble set radius 1 and crop the longer side so a square is left, e.g. left and right 0.21875 of a 16:9 video. Inspect frames with safe_area=true after.

To point at something or add a reaction, put an emoji or one of Nuzky's stickers over the video with `add_sticker(run_id, sticker, at_us)`, passing the emoji itself ("🔥", "👍🏽") rather than its name when you can; it lands centred for 3 s on a track of its own, and its `transform` moves it next to what it points at.

## Music and sound effects

When the user asks for music or a sound effect (a whoosh on a cut, a pop on a caption), find it with `search_sounds(query, kind: "music" | "effect")`: short English words such as "whoosh", "upbeat" or "cinematic". Only CC0 and CC BY ever come back, so every result is fine in a monetized video. Effects start with the ones built into Nuzky, which work offline; online results follow. Inside a run, `add_sound(run_id, id, at_us)` downloads the sound when needed and places it on an audio track at that time. Put a whoosh slightly before a cut and music from 0, then duck it under speech. When `needs_credit` is true, tell the user the video's description must credit it: the Audio panel's Copy credits and the `.credits.txt` written beside the export have the text. Offline, online_error says so; use the built-in effects or tell the user.

When the user gives a link to a song on YouTube or YouTube Music, pass the link itself as the query of `search_sounds`: it returns that one video as a music sound (`youtube:<id>`, license null) through the yt-dlp on the user's computer. Never look for songs on YouTube yourself; only the user's own link. YTDLP_MISSING: tell the user to paste the link in the Music tab of the sound library and click Download yt-dlp under it, then try again. YTDLP_OUTDATED means the yt-dlp the user installed could not get the video: ask them to do the same, as Nuzky keeps its own copy up to date. Before the first YouTube song, `add_sound` fails with RIGHTS_UNCONFIRMED: tell the user they are responsible for the rights to the music and for following YouTube's terms, and that copyrighted music can get the video muted or blocked; ask with `suggest_options`, and only when they agree call `add_sound` again with `rights_confirmed: true`. Then duck it under speech like any music; it needs no credit line from Nuzky.

For picture and sound from a YouTube link the user gave, use `add_youtube_video(run_id, link)`: it adds the whole video to the project's media. To use a part, pass both `start_us` and `end_us` in the video's own time; if the user described a moment, ask for its start and end. Optional `at_us` also places a clip there, showing only the part. The whole video downloads once (long videos can take minutes); the saved part keeps up to 3 s hidden around it that clip edges can reveal. Never find YouTube links yourself. Use the same RIGHTS_UNCONFIRMED flow as for music: explain the user's responsibility for the rights and YouTube's terms, ask with `suggest_options`, and retry with `rights_confirmed: true` only after they agree.

## Without the app

Started as `nuzky mcp --project <path> --allow-write` while the app does not have that project open, you own the project yourself: every step above works without a window, the import, transcription, cuts, captions and export_video included, and edits are saved as in the app. `nuzky new <path>` makes an empty project to start from, and `nuzky new <path> --from <CapCut draft folder>` one of the creator's CapCut draft (see Finish a draft from CapCut). Transcription needs a Whisper model the app installed; without the app nobody can be asked to download one, so MODEL_MISSING says what to tell the user. The app shows the project as busy until you exit.

AGENT_ACCESS_OFF means the user has Agent access off in Nuzky: ask them to turn it on (AI, the agent menu, Connect an agent outside Nuzky) and call again; no restart is needed. APP_NOT_RUNNING means no project is open in Nuzky: ask them to open it. APP_CLOSED from a server attached to the open project means it was closed or another one opened: read get_state again and check with the user before editing, since it may be another project now.

## Runs and low-level edits

All timeline times are integer microseconds; time ranges are half-open [start,end). Word-index ranges above are inclusive. apply_edits takes camelCase EditCmd fields, a fresh request_id per batch, and optional expected_revision/expected_speech_layout_key from get_state. edit_transcript instead uses get_transcript.transcript_key, which also guards against re-recognition. Retry identical content with the same request_id after a save failure; the live edit may already be applied. The main track is magnetic. One run is one Undo; end_run(discard) restores its start. Runs auto-keep after two idle minutes, so plan before opening one. A stopped run returns RUN_STOPPED; never resume it. undo_run works only for the last history entry in this session. To go back further, also to an earlier session, `list_history` lists the kept versions (each kept run, the user's steps, undos and restores) with index, label and short hash, and `undo_to(index)` restores one as a new step outside a run, so the versions after it stay. Ask the user before restoring over their own edits. Never change the user's selection.

## The creator's style

The style (`get_style`, also `nuzky://style`) is how this creator edits all their videos; its rules override this guide, and the rules under Your rules are the creator's own words and win over everything. When the user talks about how you should edit in general rather than this video ("never cut my sign-off", "the pauses feel too short"), or writes from the Your style page, read `get_style`, talk it through, and only once they agree make the change with `change_style`: what they tell you goes in their words as `setOwn {text}`; changing how a learned rule reads is `setText` with the whole EDIT.md and `baseVersion` from `get_style`; suggestions are theirs to `accept` or `reject`. Say in a sentence what changed; the app shows it at once and the user can undo it there. Going back to the default style is only for the user, in the app.

When a decision is the user's to make (which take to keep, tight or loose cuts, which caption style) and you run as Nuzky's AI panel, call `suggest_options(question, options: [{label, detail}])` with 2–6 short choices as the last thing in your turn and end the turn; the panel shows them as buttons and the label picked comes back as the user's next message. In a terminal, ask in plain text instead. Decide yourself whatever the guide or the creator's style already answers.

To repeat clips later in the video, such as a hook title, a sticker or B-roll 20 s on, read them with `get_state(clip_ids: [...])` and apply `{"type":"pasteClips","atUs":…,"clips":[{"clip":<the clip as get_state gives it>,"trackId":…,"trackKind":…,"keepInPlace":…}]}`, each with its track's id, kind and keepInPlace. The copies get new ids and keep their offsets to each other; each track's clips go back on it when they fit there, else on a new track right above it. Main-track clips go in back to back at the main track's cut nearest atUs, pushing the main-track clips after it along while the other tracks, except those kept in place, open the same time there, and every other clip keeps its offset to the main-track clip it starts over, so a take keeps its sound and titles. To give clips the look of another, `{"type":"pasteAttributes","source":<a clip in the same form>,"clipIds":[…]}`: text takes the style, font, transform, keyframes and animations and keeps its words (a caption its wrap width); video and images take Adjust, background, crop and shape, motion and animations, keeping their place and size; audio takes volume, fades, Voice and ducking. What the source lacks, they lose; clips of another kind are left alone and listed in skipped.

For speech recorded with audible room noise, hum or harsh s sounds, set `{"type":"updateClip","clipId":…,"cleanVoice":true}` on its clips with sound (high-pass, gentle denoise, de-ess; not for music); export waits for the cleaned sound.

Three more voice settings fix how a talking head sounds, never when; set them with the same updateClip on every clip of a take, so its pieces sound alike, and only on speech, never on music:
- Speech that rings, recorded in a bare room or far from the phone: `"dereverb":0.8` (Reduce echo; 0.5 light, 1 strongest, 0 off). At 1 it takes about 10 dB off the ringing tail from one microphone and 20 dB from two, at 0.8 about 7 and 13 dB, keeping a fifth of the original; on close, dry speech it only thins the voice, so leave it off there.
- Takes that differ in loudness, from other rooms, distances or days: `"normalize":true` (Match loudness) on all of them. Each recording then plays at -16 LUFS, measured over the whole file, so cuts within a take keep its natural ups and downs; `volume` still applies on top, for a deliberate change. Export presets level the whole video to -14 LUFS afterwards.
- A speaker who drifts within a take, turning away or leaning in: `"leveler":0.5` (Even out volume; 0.3 subtle, 0.8 strong, 0 off). It moves passages of a few seconds towards the file's loudness, never single words, so it does not pump.
For a typical phone talking head with problems: cleanVoice for noise, dereverb for a ringing room, leveler for drifting level, normalize to match takes. Each file's sound is prepared once in the background; export_video waits for it or prepares it itself.

To blur or replace what is behind a talking head, set `{"type":"updateClip","clipId":…,"background":{"type":"blur","strength":0.5}}` on every clip of it, or `{"type":"color","color":"#1c1c1e"}`, `{"type":"image","assetId":…}` of an imported image, `{"type":"none"}` to undo; a colour with alpha 00 leaves only the person over the tracks below. The person is found once per file in the background: inspect_frames answers MATTE_NOT_READY with a job to wait on with `job` action `wait`, export_video does it itself. When the face and person models are missing, the open Nuzky app asks the user while the job waits (phase `waiting_for_user`, then `downloading_vision_models`); keep waiting. MODEL_DECLINED means the user said not now or stopped it: do not call tools needing those models again, carry on without them and mention that Get set up downloads them. Without the app MODEL_MISSING says what to tell the user. It suits one person filling much of the frame; check the edges with inspect_frames.

To speed up a talking head, 1.1–1.25 is common: `{"type":"updateClip","clipId":…,"speed":1.2}`. A clip moved off 1x keeps the pitch of its voice (`keepPitch` turns on); add `"keepPitch":false` only when the user wants the voice higher or lower with the speed. Captions and word times follow the speed either way.

For music under speech, set `{"type":"updateClip","clipId":…,"duckDb":12}` on the music clip instead of lowering its volume by hand: it goes 12 dB down while a video's own sound has speech and comes back in the pauses, the same in playback and export. Speech is any sound in video clips above -40 dBFS, never an imported audio file. 6 is gentle, 18 strong, up to 40; 0 turns it off.
